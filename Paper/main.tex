\begin{abstract}
Stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) has a discretization error of first order in the step size $h$, even with the full gradient. We study the stochastic gradient Leimkuhler--Matthews (SGLM) integrator, which has the same cost as SGLD, one stochastic gradient per step, and reuses each Gaussian vector in consecutive steps. For a potential strongly convex outside a ball and a Lipschitz test function, we prove the bound $\mathcal O(\frac{1}{Nh} + \Sigma^2 h^2 + h^4 \log^2\frac1h)$ on the mean square error (MSE) of time averages of SGLM over $N$ steps, where $\Sigma$ bounds the variance of the gradient noise. The proof uses a modified class that absorbs the non-Markovian part of SGLM. The discretization error enters this bound as $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ and the MSE bound of SGLD as $h^2$, while a larger batch reduces only the term $\Sigma^2 h^2$ in both bounds. In Bayesian logistic regression on MNIST, SGLM has a smaller estimated MSE than SGLD. The coefficient of $h$ in the numerical bias of SGLM is given explicitly by the covariance of the gradient noise and the continuous Poisson equation. The coefficient for SGLD contains in addition the Hessian of the potential, which comes from the discretization error. 

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} overdamped Langevin dynamics, stochastic gradient, Leimkuhler--Matthews integrator, mean square error, discrete Poisson equation, numerical bias, Riesz representor, Wasserstein-1 distance.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{MSC 2020:} 65C30, 60H35, 65C40.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec: intro}

Sampling from the Boltzmann distribution $\pi(x) \propto e^{-U(x)}$ on $\mathbb R^d$ is a central task in statistical physics \citep{lelievre2016partial, leimkuhler2015molecular} and in Bayesian inference \citep{welling2011bayesian, dalalyan2017user, durmus2017nonasymptotic}. The simplest stochastic differential equation that leaves $\pi$ invariant is the overdamped Langevin dynamics
\begin{equation}
    \D X_t = -\nabla U(X_t)\,\D t + \sqrt2\,\D B_t , \qquad X_0 = x \in \mathbb R^d ,
    \label{eq: langevin}
\end{equation}
where $(B_t)_{t \Ge 0}$ is a standard Brownian motion in $\mathbb R^d$ and $U$ a confining potential; time averages along the solution $(X_t)_{t \Ge 0}$ approximate expectations under $\pi$ in the long-time limit \citep{roberts1996exponential, lamberton2002recursive}. A numerical integrator of \eqref{eq: langevin} with step size $h > 0$ produces samples $(Z_k)_{k \Ge 0}$ that approximate the invariant distribution $\pi$; such integrators form the basis of Langevin Monte Carlo \citep{dalalyan2017user, durmus2017nonasymptotic, cheng2018sharp, durmus2019high, vempala2019rapid, majka2020nonasymptotic}. Characterizing the accuracy of these samples is a basic question of numerical analysis.

The mean square error (MSE) of time averages is a natural criterion for this accuracy. For a test function $f$, with $\pi(f) := \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\,\D\pi$, the MSE of the time average over $N$ steps is
\begin{equation}
    \MSE(N, h) := \mathbb E \Biggl[ \biggl| \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} f(Z_k) - \pi(f) \biggr|^2 \Biggr] .
    \label{eq: mse}
\end{equation}
The Wasserstein distance, the Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence and the total variation distance compare the law of the samples with $\pi$, and are hard to evaluate in high dimension. The MSE instead concerns a single numerical trajectory, and accounts for both the fluctuation of the time average and the deviation of its long-time limit from $\pi(f)$, the numerical bias \citep{mattingly2010convergence, chen2015convergence, teh2016consistency, vollmer2016exploration}.

In Bayesian inference on large data sets, $\nabla U$ is replaced by an unbiased stochastic estimator $\hat g(x; \xi)$ computed from a mini-batch, with $\xi$ a random variable independent of the Brownian motion \citep{welling2011bayesian, chen2015convergence, teh2016consistency, vollmer2016exploration}. The standard sampler of this kind is stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) \citep{welling2011bayesian}, the Euler--Maruyama (EM) integrator \citep{kloeden1992numerical, milstein2004stochastic} of \eqref{eq: langevin} with the stochastic gradient,
\begin{equation}
    Z_{k+1} = Z_k - h\, \hat g(Z_k; \xi_k) + \sqrt{2h}\, \chi_{k+1} ,
    \label{eq: sgld}
    \tag{SGLD}
\end{equation}
where $(\chi_k)_{k \Ge 0}$ is a sequence of independent standard Gaussian vectors in $\mathbb R^d$ and $\xi_k$ is drawn afresh at every step. The discretization error of \eqref{eq: sgld} is of first order in $h$: its numerical bias is of order $h$ even with the full gradient (Section~\ref{subsec: related sgld}). The existing stochastic gradient integrators that preserve a second order discretization error are built on stochastic gradient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (SGHMC) \citep{chen2014stochastic} with a symmetric splitting integrator \citep{chen2015convergence} or on the underdamped Langevin dynamics \citep{gouraud2025hmc}, whose splitting integrators are of second order with one gradient evaluation per step \citep{monmarche2021high}. The underdamped dynamics with stochastic gradients is studied in \citep{leimkuhler2016adaptive, matthews2018langevin, paulin2025sampling, lu2025mse}.

We study the \emph{stochastic gradient Leimkuhler--Matthews} (SGLM) integrator, which has the cost of \eqref{eq: sgld}, one stochastic gradient per step, and differs from it in only one respect: the Gaussian vector $\chi_{k+1}$ is replaced by the average of $\chi_k$ and $\chi_{k+1}$, so that each Gaussian vector is used twice, in consecutive steps,
\begin{equation}
    Z_{k+1} = Z_k - h\, \hat g(Z_k; \xi_k) + \sqrt{\frac h2}\, \bigl( \chi_k + \chi_{k+1} \bigr) .
    \label{eq: sglm}
    \tag{SGLM}
\end{equation}
With the full gradient, SGLM is the Leimkuhler--Matthews (LM) integrator \citep{leimkuhler2013rational, vilmart2015postprocessed},
\begin{equation}
    Z_{k+1} = Z_k - h\, \nabla U(Z_k) + \sqrt{\frac h2}\, \bigl( \chi_k + \chi_{k+1} \bigr) .
    \label{eq: lm}
    \tag{LM}
\end{equation}
The reuse of the Gaussian vector gives LM a second order discretization error for the invariant distribution $\pi$ \citep{leimkuhler2013rational, leimkuhler2014long, vilmart2015postprocessed}, although its samples $(Z_k)_{k \Ge 0}$ do not form a Markov chain and its weak error on finite time intervals is of first order. LM evaluates $\nabla U$ once per step, while almost all other integrators of \eqref{eq: langevin} with a second order discretization error are of predictor--corrector type, with at least two sequential evaluations of $\nabla U$ per step (Section~\ref{subsec: related lm}). With a stochastic gradient, each of these evaluations carries its own noise. This raises the question whether SGLM preserves the second order discretization error of LM.

We provide a positive answer to this question. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

\subsubsection*{1. SGLM separates the stochastic gradient error from the discretization error.} For a Lipschitz test function $f$, under regularity conditions on $U$ and the stochastic gradient that require strong convexity of $U$ only outside a ball, we prove in Theorem~\ref{thm: sglm mse} the MSE bound for SGLM,
\begin{flalign}
	\makebox[0.3\linewidth][l]{(SGLM, variance $\Sigma$)} &
	\MSE(N, h) \Le C\norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2 \Bigl( \frac{1}{Nh} + \Sigma^2 h^2 + h^4 \log^2\frac1h \Bigr) , &&
	\label{eq: intro sg mse}
\end{flalign}
where $\Sigma$ bounds the variance of the stochastic gradient, and $C$ depends on the regularity constants of $U$, the dimension $d$ and the initial value $Z_0$, but not on $N$, $h$ or $\Sigma$. In \eqref{eq: intro sg mse}, $\frac{1}{Nh}$ comes from the fluctuation of the time average, $\Sigma^2 h^2$ from the square of the stochastic gradient error, and $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ from the square of the discretization error. The same separation holds for the UBU integrator with stochastic gradients (SG-UBU) \citep{lu2025mse}, and was previously known only for integrators of the underdamped dynamics \citep{lu2025mse, paulin2026theoretical}. The \eqref{eq: sgld} integrator does not have it, since its stochastic gradient error and its discretization error are both of first order in $h$. With the full gradient, $\Sigma = 0$ and \eqref{eq: intro sg mse} yields the MSE bound $\frac{1}{Nh} + h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ of LM. To our knowledge, this is also the first MSE bound for the LM integrator.

The separation in \eqref{eq: intro sg mse} is advantageous in the mini-batch regime: if each step averages $P$ independent stochastic gradients, the variance bound $\Sigma$ is replaced by $\Sigma/P$, so the contribution of the gradient noise to the MSE becomes $\Sigma^2 h^2 / P^2$, with $C$ in \eqref{eq: intro sg mse} independent of $P$. Increasing the batch size $P$ at a fixed step size $h$ therefore reduces the stochastic gradient error. By contrast, the MSE of \eqref{eq: sgld} keeps the term $h^2$ from the square of its discretization error at every batch size, and only a smaller $h$ reduces it. Since the gradients of a batch are evaluated in parallel while the steps are sequential, SGLM with a large batch size $P$ has a smaller error bound than SGLD under the same cost.

The proof of \eqref{eq: intro sg mse} runs on the Markovian form of \eqref{eq: sglm}, a Markov chain $(Y_k)_{k \Ge 0}$ with $Z_k = Y_k + \sqrt{h/2}\,\chi_k$. It builds on the discrete Poisson equation of our earlier work \citep{lu2025mse, ye2026mse}, combined with a modified class of test function, potential, generator and discrete Poisson solution that absorbs the non-Markovian part of the integrator, the Gaussian shift $\sqrt{h/2}\,\chi_k$.

\subsubsection*{2. The coefficients of the numerical bias are explicit.} The time averages of $f$ along \eqref{eq: sglm} converge almost surely to $\pi_h(f)$, for a numerical invariant distribution $\pi_h$ independent of the initial value, and the numerical bias $\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)$ is of order $h$ with an explicit coefficient: by Theorem~\ref{thm: numerical bias}, for every Lipschitz test function $f$,
\begin{flalign}
	\makebox[0.33\linewidth][l]{(SGLM, numerical bias)} &
	\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)}{h} = \frac12\, \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \mathcal C(x) \bigr) , &&
	\label{eq: intro bias coefficient}
\end{flalign}
where $\mathcal C(x)$ is the covariance of the gradient noise $\hat g(x;\xi) - \nabla U(x)$, $\phi_0$ solves the continuous Poisson equation $-\mathcal L \phi_0 = f - \pi(f)$ with $\mathcal L$ the generator of \eqref{eq: langevin}, and $:$ is the full contraction of two matrices. The difference between $\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)$ and $h$ times this coefficient is at most $C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^2 \log\frac1h$. The coefficient comes entirely from the gradient noise: with the full gradient, $\mathcal C(x) = 0$ and the numerical bias of LM has no linear part. In the proof, the gradient noise enters only through its covariance $\mathcal C(x)$ in the local weak error of a single step, an approach similar to that of SG-UBU in \citep[Section~3.4]{lu2025mse}.

The discrete Poisson equation also characterizes the numerical bias of \eqref{eq: sgld}. The time averages of SGLD converge to $\bar\pi_h(f)$, with $\bar\pi_h$ its invariant distribution, and by Theorem~\ref{thm: sgld bias},
\begin{flalign}
	\makebox[0.28\linewidth][l]{(SGLD, numerical bias)} &
	\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\bar\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)}{h} = \frac12\, \pi\Bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \bigl( \nabla^2 U(x) + \mathcal C(x) \bigr) \Bigr) , &&
	\label{eq: intro sgld coefficient}
\end{flalign}
a coefficient studied formally for $d = 1$ in \citep{vollmer2016exploration}. Besides the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$, the coefficient of SGLD carries the Hessian $\nabla^2 U(x)$, which comes from the discretization error of EM. With the full gradient, $\mathcal C(x) = 0$ and \eqref{eq: intro sgld coefficient} gives the coefficient of EM; with a batch of $P$ independent stochastic gradients, the coefficient of SGLM is divided by $P$, while that of SGLD tends to the coefficient of EM as $P$ grows. In \eqref{eq: intro bias coefficient} and \eqref{eq: intro sgld coefficient}, the gradient noise and the discretization error both enter linearly through $\nabla^2 \phi_0$, so the solution $\phi_0$ of the continuous Poisson equation is an essential structure of the numerical bias of the two stochastic gradient integrators, not only a tool for upper bounds on the MSE.

\subsubsection*{3. The coefficients of the Wasserstein-1 error are explicit.} The suprema of the absolute values of the coefficients \eqref{eq: intro bias coefficient} and \eqref{eq: intro sgld coefficient} over the test functions with $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty \Le 1$ are the limits of the normalized Wasserstein-1 errors $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)/h$ and $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)/h$ as $h \to 0$ (Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits}):
\begin{flalign}
	\makebox[0.35\linewidth][l]{(SGLM, $\mathcal W_1$ coefficient)} &
	\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)}{h} = \frac12\, \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} , &&
	\label{eq: intro w1 sglm} \\
	\makebox[0.35\linewidth][l]{(SGLD, $\mathcal W_1$ coefficient)} &
	\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)}{h} = \frac12\, \norm{( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} )\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} , &&
	\label{eq: intro w1 sgld}
\end{flalign}
where the Riesz representors $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ and $\psi_{\nabla^2 U}$ of $\mathcal C(x)$ and $\nabla^2 U(x)$ are the functions, independent of $f$, with $\pi( \nabla^2 \phi_0 : \mathcal C ) = \pi( f \psi_{\mathcal C} )$ and $\pi( \nabla^2 \phi_0 : \nabla^2 U ) = \pi( f \psi_{\nabla^2 U} )$, and $\norm{\nu}_{\mathrm{KR}}$ is the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm of a signed measure $\nu$, the supremum of $\int f\, \D\nu$ over the test functions with $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty \Le 1$. Since Theorems~\ref{thm: numerical bias} and \ref{thm: sgld bias} hold for every Lipschitz test function, the normalized biases $(\pi_h - \pi)/h$ and $(\bar\pi_h - \pi)/h$ converge to $\frac12 \psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi$ and $\frac12 (\psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C})\, \pi$. SGLM does not always have a smaller coefficient of the Wasserstein-1 error than SGLD. With a batch of $P$ independent stochastic gradients, $\psi_{\mathcal C}/P$ replaces $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ in both limits, with $\mathcal C(x)$ the covariance of a single stochastic gradient. For a strongly convex potential, the coefficient of SGLM is at most that of SGLD once the batch size reaches a threshold linear in $\sup_x \norm{\mathcal C(x)}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm: batch threshold}).

\subsubsection*{Summary of the results}

Three features distinguish these results. First, SGLM separates the stochastic gradient error from the discretization error at the cost of SGLD, so that the batch size and the step size control the two errors independently; for an integrator of the overdamped dynamics, this separation is new. Second, the results on the numerical bias are equalities, not only upper bounds: the mainstream coupling methods bound the Wasserstein-1 errors from above without identifying their coefficients. Third, the exact coefficients make the comparison of SGLM with SGLD precise, with, for a strongly convex potential, an explicit threshold of the batch size beyond which the Wasserstein-1 error of SGLM is at most that of SGLD, to first order in $h$.

As applications of the analysis of the coefficients, Section~\ref{sec: representors} describes three approaches to bias correction. The numerical bias of SGLM is $\frac h2\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ to first order in $h$, so it is removed once $\pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ is computed. The first approach computes it with the explicit representor $\psi_{\mathcal C}$, available for some covariances, and the second by Richardson--Romberg extrapolation in the step size. The third approach, the shifting correction, replaces a sample $Z \sim \pi_h$ of SGLM by $Z + h \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}(Z)$ for a function $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$, and formally removes the first order bias coefficient for all test functions at once. The function $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$ is approximated by minimizing an energy that requires only the samples of SGLM at the step sizes $h$ and $2h$, so the shifting correction is data-driven.

In the numerical experiments of Section~\ref{sec: numerics}, the evaluated coefficients of the numerical bias of SGLM and SGLD in a 4D Gaussian mixture distribution approach the exact coefficients \eqref{eq: intro bias coefficient} and \eqref{eq: intro sgld coefficient} as $h$ decreases, and the evaluated coefficients of the Wasserstein-1 errors in a radial distribution approach those of \eqref{eq: intro w1 sglm} and \eqref{eq: intro w1 sgld}. In a 16D product of ring distributions, the approaches to bias correction of Section~\ref{sec: representors} reduce the numerical bias of SGLM. In Bayesian logistic regression on MNIST, SGLM has a smaller estimated MSE than SGLD with the same step size and batch size.

\section{Related works}
\label{sec: related}

\subsection{Second order integrators in the overdamped case}
\label{subsec: related lm}

Among the integrators of \eqref{eq: langevin} that evaluate $\nabla U$ alone, only a few have a discretization error of order $h^2$ for the invariant distribution. They include LM \citep{leimkuhler2013rational, vilmart2015postprocessed}, stochastic Heun \citep{milstein2004stochastic, debrabant2010runge, mannella2025numerical}, and members of the large family of stochastic Runge--Kutta (SRK) integrators \citep{rossler2010runge, debrabant2010runge, li2019stochastic, yang2026accelerating}, such as SRK-I/II \citep{yang2026accelerating, ye2026mse}. Beyond LM, almost all these integrators are of predictor--corrector type: the second evaluation of $\nabla U$ in a step is taken at a state computed from the first. These integrators are compared in detail in \citep{ye2026mse}. Integrators of high order that also use derivatives of $\nabla U$ are constructed in \citep{talay1990second, abdulle2014high}.

The LM integrator was introduced in \citep{leimkuhler2013rational} as the high friction limit of the BAOAB splitting of the underdamped Langevin dynamics. Its second order discretization error for the invariant distribution was proved by Talay--Tubaro expansions \citep{talay1990expansion}. These expansions were derived for the weak error, whose first order coefficient decays exponentially in time \citep{leimkuhler2014long}, and for the invariant distribution of splitting integrators on the torus \citep{leimkuhler2015computation}. The second order discretization error of LM was also proved by writing the integrator as its Markovian form followed by the Gaussian shift $\sqrt{h/2}\,\chi_k$ as a postprocessor \citep{vilmart2015postprocessed}. The postprocessor argument uses the sufficient conditions for high order on the invariant distribution of \citep{abdulle2014high}, which rest on the Talay--Tubaro expansion \citep{talay1990expansion} and on the weak backward error analysis of \citep{debussche2012weak, kopec2013weak}. In high dimension and under weak or sparse interactions among the variables, the bias of the unadjusted underdamped Langevin algorithm on marginals of a few coordinates depends on the dimension only through logarithmic factors \citep{chen2026delocalization}, extending the delocalization of bias of the unadjusted overdamped Langevin algorithm \citep{chen2026convergence}. This delocalization holds for all large friction parameters and therefore carries over to \eqref{eq: lm}.
These works control the numerical bias of LM; none of them bounds the MSE \eqref{eq: mse} or treats stochastic gradients.

The modified class of this paper serves a purpose different from that of the modified equations of \citep{abdulle2014high}, which are used to construct integrators. Here the integrator is fixed, and the modified potential defines a modified dynamics, against which the Markovian form of LM has a local weak error of order $h^3$; the MSE analysis of \citep{lu2025mse, ye2026mse} is applied to the discrete Poisson solution of this dynamics. The order $h^3$ of this local weak error appears in the Talay--Tubaro expansions \citep{leimkuhler2014long, leimkuhler2015computation}, where the coefficient of order $h$ vanishes at equilibrium, and in the postprocessor argument \citep{vilmart2015postprocessed}. The expansions in \citep{leimkuhler2014long} rely on the exponential decay in time of the derivatives of the solution of the Kolmogorov equation of \eqref{eq: langevin}, for which they refer to \citep{talay1990second}. Theorem~\ref{thm: u decay} proves this decay on $\mathbb R^d$ for a potential strongly convex outside a ball and a Lipschitz test function.

\subsection{Sampling error analysis for SGLD}
\label{subsec: related sgld}

The \eqref{eq: sgld} integrator converges weakly to the solution of \eqref{eq: langevin} as $h$ tends to zero \citep{sato2014approximation}. Its sampling error has been analyzed along the following lines, and Table~\ref{tab: sgld} collects representative bounds; see \citep{nemeth2021stochastic} for a review.
\begin{itemize}
	\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
	\item Recurrence relations of the integrator, combined with strong or weak error estimates, give nonasymptotic bounds in Wasserstein distance \citep{dalalyan2017user, nagapetyan2017true, brosse2018promises, barkhagen2021stochastic, chau2021stochastic, zhang2023nonasymptotic, shaw2025random}. Weighted transportation cost inequalities give bounds in $\mathcal W_2$ for nonconvex potentials \citep{raginsky2017nonconvex}. Under log-concavity at infinity the variance of the stochastic gradient does not change the rate of weak convergence \citep{majka2020nonasymptotic}, and a conductance analysis gives bounds in total variation for targets that are not log-concave \citep{zou2021faster}. For targets log-concave outside a compact set, the law of the $k$-th iterate of SGLD converges geometrically in $\mathcal W_1$ to the numerical invariant distribution $\bar\pi_h$ of SGLD \citep{li2024geometric}.
	\item The continuous Poisson equation controls the error of the time averages of numerical schemes \citep{mattingly2010convergence, brosse2019tamed} and of SGLD and SGHMC, including their MSE \citep{chen2015convergence, teh2016consistency, vollmer2016exploration, xu2018global, li2023improving}. These results require its solution $\phi_0$ to have bounded or polynomially growing derivatives.
	\item The KL divergence between the laws of SGLD and of \eqref{eq: langevin} is bounded uniformly in time \citep{li2022sharp}. Bounds in KL divergence also follow from convex optimization on the Wasserstein space \citep{durmus2019analysis} and, under a logarithmic Sobolev inequality, from the nearly Gaussian structure of the gradient noise \citep{das2023utilising}.
	\item Variance-reduced and control-variate estimators of the gradient improve these guarantees \citep{dubey2016variance, chatterji2018theory, baker2019control, zou2019sampling, kinoshita2022improved}. The numerical bias of SGLD is reduced by Richardson--Romberg extrapolation \citep{durmus2016stochastic}, and its cost at a prescribed accuracy is reduced by multilevel Monte Carlo \citep{giles2016multilevel, giles2020multilevel}. The extrapolation, however, introduces negative weights and does not increase the accuracy of the trajectory itself. The cost of the gradient noise is quantified by oracle lower bounds \citep{chatterji2022oracle}, by the dependence of the MSE on the mini-batch size under a fixed budget \citep{chen2019convergence}, and by a comparison of SGLD with EM at a prescribed mean square accuracy \citep{majka2026computational}.
\end{itemize}

The numerical bias of \eqref{eq: sgld} is of first order in $h$. The bias of the time average over $N$ steps is $\mathcal O\bigl( (Nh)^{-1} + h \bigr)$ \citep[Theorem~9]{vollmer2016exploration}. The uniform-in-time KL bound of \citep[Corollary~3.1]{li2022sharp}, of order $h^2$, combined with the geometric ergodicity of SGLD for a potential strongly convex outside a ball \citep{li2024geometric}, yields $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi) \Le Ch$ \citep[Section~5.1]{li2022sharp}. Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits} gives the exact coefficients of $h$ in $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)$ and $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)$. For a strongly convex potential with a Lipschitz gradient and gradient noise that is Lipschitz in mean square, $\mathcal W_2(\bar\pi_h, \pi) \Le Ch$ when the gradient noise has a finite moment of order $4$, while under a finite moment of order $2$ only, the bound is of order $h^{\frac12}$ \citep[Theorem~1]{paulin2026sgld}. A family of spiked gradient noises shows that the order $h^{\frac12}$ cannot be improved uniformly over gradient noises of a fixed variance \citep[Lemma~1]{paulin2026sgld}. Each noise of this family has finite moments of all orders, while the moments of order $12$ are unbounded over the family; our uniform bound on the moment of order $12$ of the stochastic gradient excludes the family as a whole. The first order term of the numerical bias has two sources, the gradient noise and the discretization error of EM, which gives a numerical bias of first order even with the full gradient \citep{talay1990expansion}; see also \citep[Section~4.1]{vollmer2016exploration}.

A different line of work removes the numerical bias. The proximal sampler \citep{lee2021structured, chen2022improved}, with its restricted Gaussian oracle implemented by first order rejection sampling \citep{chen2026high}, reaches a prescribed accuracy in total variation with a number of gradient queries polylogarithmic in the inverse accuracy \citep{altschuler2024faster, chen2026high}, and stochastic gradients with subexponential tails preserve this rate for targets satisfying a logarithmic Sobolev or a Poincar\'e inequality \citep{chen2026stochastic}. These samplers need a warm start, an approximate proximal step and a rejection step with a random number of gradient queries in each iteration, and light-tailed gradient noise: with stochastic gradients of bounded variance only, a number of queries proportional to the inverse accuracy is necessary and sufficient \citep{chen2026stochastic}. SGLD and SGLM use one stochastic gradient per step and no warm start, and their numerical bias, quantified in the present paper, can be made smaller than the fluctuation of the time average by the choice of the step size and the batch size.

\subsection{Stochastic gradient integrators for underdamped Langevin dynamics}
\label{subsec: related underdamped}

The underdamped Langevin dynamics augments the position with a velocity and is discretized by splitting integrators such as BAOAB \citep{leimkuhler2013rational, leimkuhler2015molecular}, OBABO \citep{bussi2007accurate} and UBU \citep{sanz2021wasserstein, paulin2024correction}. For convex and smooth potentials, the unadjusted chain of a splitting integrator of second order with one gradient evaluation per step contracts in Wasserstein distance at a rate independent of the dimension \citep{monmarche2021high}.

SGHMC adds a friction to compensate for the gradient noise \citep{chen2014stochastic}, within a general recipe for such samplers \citep{ma2015complete}. Adaptive thermostats correct for the gradient noise \citep{ding2014bayesian, leimkuhler2016adaptive, shang2015covariance}, with exponential convergence of the law \citep{leimkuhler2020hypocoercivity} and a quantified bias from mini-batching \citep{sekkat2023minibatching}. Variance reduction accelerates SGHMC \citep{li2019variance, zou2018stochastic, zou2019stochastic, chatterji2018theory, zou2021convergence}, and multilevel coupling gives unbiased estimators \citep{chada2026unbiased}. For nonconvex optimization, the complexity bounds of SGHMC can be tighter than those of \eqref{eq: sgld} \citep{gao2022global}. Wasserstein bounds are proved without log-concavity \citep{akyildiz2024nonasymptotic, chak2023reflection}, through contraction rates of stochastic gradient integrators \citep{leimkuhler2023contraction}, and with dimension-free rates for log-concave targets \citep{gouraud2025hmc}.

Consider a stochastic gradient sampler such as SGHMC, with unbiased gradients and a Markov integrator whose local weak error is of order $h^{K+1}$. If the derivatives of the solution of the continuous Poisson equation are bounded, the time average of this sampler has a bias of order $(Nh)^{-1} + h^K$ and an MSE of order $(Nh)^{-1} + h^{2K}$ \citep[Theorems~2 and~3]{chen2015convergence}. Applied to the Markovian form of LM, which has weak order $1$ \citep{ye2026mse}, this framework gives a discretization term of order $h^2$ in the MSE, whereas the modified class gives $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$.

For SG-UBU and a globally convex potential, the discrete Poisson equation bounds the MSE of the time average \citep{lu2025mse}. The numerical bias is of first order in $h$, with a coefficient proportional to the variance of the stochastic gradient. For a finite-sum potential whose components are each strongly convex, UBU combined with a symmetric forward and backward sweep over the mini-batches reaches a bias of order $h^2$ with a single mini-batch per step \citep{paulin2025sampling}. The constant of this bound grows with the number of mini-batches, and the order rests on the symmetry of the whole sweep, which lasts a time equal to $2h$ times the number of mini-batches. For SG-UBU and strongly log-concave targets, Gaussian convolution inequalities bound the Wasserstein distance between the numerical invariant distribution and $\pi$ \citep{paulin2026theoretical}. The bound is the discretization error of the UBU integrator plus a stochastic gradient error of first order in $h$, which vanishes with the full gradient.

Like SG-UBU, \eqref{eq: sglm} evaluates one gradient per step and has a discretization error of second order, and the separation in \eqref{eq: intro sg mse} is comparable with that of SG-UBU in \citep{lu2025mse}. Unlike the sweep of \citep{paulin2025sampling}, SGLM draws $\xi_k$ independently, so its numerical bias keeps the first order term \eqref{eq: intro bias coefficient} from the gradient noise. Like the first order term of SG-UBU, this term comes from the gradient noise only and vanishes with the full gradient, whereas the coefficient \eqref{eq: intro sgld coefficient} of \eqref{eq: sgld} also carries the Hessian $\nabla^2 U(x)$ from the discretization error of EM. UBU and LM are analyzed differently: UBU has strong error of second order \citep{sanz2021wasserstein, paulin2024correction}, whereas the Markovian form of LM has strong error of first order \citep{ye2026mse}, and its second order discretization error is recovered through the modified class.

\subsection{Notation and assumptions}
\label{subsec: notation}

Throughout, $\langle \cdot , \cdot \rangle$ is the Euclidean inner product, $\norm{\,\cdot\,}$ the operator norm of a tensor, which for a vector is the Euclidean norm $|\cdot|$, $I_d$ is the $d \times d$ identity matrix, $A \succeq B$ means that $A - B$ is positive semidefinite, $v^{\otimes j}$ is the $j$-fold tensor power, $A : B$ the full contraction of two tensors of the same order, $\mathrm{Tr}[A]$ the trace of a square matrix $A$, and $\mathrm{Sym}\, T$ the average of a tensor $T$ over the permutations of its indices. For a matrix $Q$ and a vector $v$, $\nabla^3 U(x)[Q] \in \mathbb R^d$ and $\nabla^3 U(x)[v] \in \mathbb R^{d\times d}$ have entries
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl( \nabla^3 U(x)[Q] \bigr)_i = \sum_{j,k=1}^d \partial_i\partial_j\partial_k U(x)\, Q_{jk} , \qquad
    \bigl( \nabla^3 U(x)[v] \bigr)_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^d \partial_i\partial_j\partial_k U(x)\, v_k .
\end{equation*}
We write $\Delta U = \sum_i \partial_i^2 U$ and $\nabla \Delta U$ for its gradient, $\mathbb E^x$ for the expectation of the Langevin dynamics \eqref{eq: langevin} started at $X_0 = x$, and $\mathbb E_\xi$ and $\mathbb P_\xi$ for the expectation and the probability over the random variable $\xi$ of the stochastic gradient at fixed $x$. For a tensor-valued function $\psi$ and $j \Ge 0$, $\norm{\nabla^j \psi}_\infty := \sup_x \norm{\nabla^j \psi(x)}$; for a Lipschitz function $f$, $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$ is its Lipschitz constant. The heat semigroup is
\begin{equation*}
	(e^{t\Delta} f)(x) := \mathbb E\Bigl[ f( x + \sqrt{2t}\, \chi ):\chi \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d) \Bigr] = \frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac d2}} \int_{\mathbb R^d} f(y) \exp\Bigl( -\frac{|x-y|^2}{4t} \Bigr) \D y .
\end{equation*}
For a random tensor $X$ and $p \Ge 1$, $\pnorm{X}_p := ( \mathbb E \norm{X}^p )^{\frac1p}$, with the Euclidean norm $|X|$ in place of $\norm{X}$ when $X$ is a vector. Most proofs do not track the dependence of the constants on the dimension $d$, so the norms used in the paper are equivalent, up to constants depending only on $d$.

For a signed measure $\nu$ with $\nu(\mathbb R^d) = 0$ and $\int |x|\, |\nu|(\D x) < \infty$, the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm is
\begin{equation*}
    \norm{\nu}_{\mathrm{KR}} := \sup_{\norm{\nabla f}_\infty \Le 1} \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \D\nu ,
\end{equation*}
with the supremum over the Lipschitz functions $f$ with $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty \Le 1$, so that $\mathcal W_1(\mu, \rho) = \norm{\mu - \rho}_{\mathrm{KR}}$ for probability measures $\mu$ and $\rho$ with finite first moments by the Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality. For a function $\psi$ with $\pi(\psi) = 0$ and $\pi(|x|\,|\psi|) < \infty$, $\psi\pi$ is the signed measure $\psi(x)\, \pi(\D x)$.

Three conventions fix the notation of the paper. The solution of the discrete Poisson equation of \eqref{eq: langevin} is written $\phi_h$, the subscript $h$ marking its dependence on the step size. Every member of the modified class of Section~\ref{sec: modified} carries a tilde, and Table~\ref{tab: notation} lists the objects of \eqref{eq: langevin} and their modified counterparts. The hat marks the stochastic gradient: $\hat g(x;\xi)$ is the estimator of $\nabla U(x)$.

We make the following assumption on $U$. Strong convexity of $U$ is required only outside a ball.

\begin{assumption}
	\label{ass: U}
	The potential $U \in C^7(\mathbb R^d)$ satisfies
	\begin{equation*}
		|\nabla U(0)| \Le M , \qquad \max_{2 \Le i \Le 7}\, \norm{\nabla^i U(x)} \Le M , \qquad \forall x \in \mathbb R^d ,
	\end{equation*}
	and there are constants $m > 0$ and $R \Ge 0$ such that $\nabla^2 U(x) \succeq m I_d$ for $|x| \Ge R$.
\end{assumption}

For \eqref{eq: sglm}, boundedness conditions are required for the stochastic gradient $\hat g(x;\xi)$.

\begin{assumption}
	\label{ass: sg}
	The stochastic gradient $\hat g(\cdot;\xi) \in C^1(\mathbb R^d; \mathbb R^d)$ is unbiased, $\mathbb E_\xi\big[\hat g(x;\xi)\big] = \nabla U(x)$, and the map $(x,\xi) \mapsto \nabla \hat g(x;\xi)$ is measurable.
	With the constant $M$ of Assumption~\ref{ass: U},
	\begin{equation*}
		\Big( \mathbb E_\xi \big[ |\hat g(x;\xi)|^{12} \big] \Big)^{\frac{1}{12}} \Le M \bigl( 1 + |x| \bigr) , \qquad
		\mathbb E_\xi \bigg[ \sup_{|x'-x| \Le 1} \norm{\nabla \hat g(x';\xi)}^2 \bigg] \Le M^2 , \qquad
		\forall x \in \mathbb R^d ,
	\end{equation*}
	and the variance is bounded by a constant $\Sigma \Le M^2$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\mathbb E_\xi \big[ |\hat g(x;\xi) - \nabla U(x)|^2 \big] \Le \Sigma , \qquad
		\forall x \in \mathbb R^d .
	\end{equation*}
\end{assumption}

The constraint $\Sigma \Le M^2$ in Assumption~\ref{ass: sg} is imposed for the convenience of analysis. The bound on the derivative $\nabla \hat g$ in Assumption~\ref{ass: sg} ensures the uniqueness of the invariant distribution of \eqref{eq: sglm markov}, as required by Harris's ergodic theorem \citep{hairer2008yet}. A stronger condition is the almost sure uniform bound $\norm{\nabla \hat g(x;\xi)} \Le M$ for all $x \in \mathbb R^d$ and almost every $\xi$. The full gradient $\hat g(x;\xi) = \nabla U(x)$ satisfies Assumption~\ref{ass: sg} with $\Sigma = 0$, so every result below on \eqref{eq: sglm} applies to \eqref{eq: lm} with $\Sigma = 0$.

From here on, the letter $C$ denotes a positive constant depending only on $m$, $M$, $R$ and $d$, but not on $h$, $N$, $P$, $\Sigma$ or the initial state, and it changes from line to line. The letter $h_0$ denotes an upper bound in $(0,\frac12]$ on the step size $h$, depending only on $m$, $M$, $R$ and $d$. In each statement, $h_0$ is the smallest of the bounds its proof uses, and every statement below holds for $h \in (0,h_0]$. The notation $\mathcal O(\cdot)$ denotes a term bounded by $C$ times its argument.

\subsection{Markovian form of SGLM}

The \eqref{eq: sglm} integrator is written in a Markovian form for the convenience of analysis. We realize the Gaussian vectors as the increments of the Brownian motion $(B_t)_{t \Ge 0}$ of \eqref{eq: langevin} over the steps,
\begin{equation*}
	\chi_k = \frac1{\sqrt{h}}\big(B_{(k+1)h} - B_{kh}\big), \qquad k \Ge 0 ,
\end{equation*}
and the random variables $\xi_k$ are independent and identically distributed and independent of $(B_t)_{t \Ge 0}$. Let $\mathcal F_k$ be the sigma algebra generated by the Brownian motion up to the time $kh$ and by $\xi_0, \dots, \xi_{k-1}$, the past before the step $k$, and $\mathbb E_k$ the conditional expectation given that past,
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal F_k := \sigma\bigl( B_s ,\ \xi_j : 0 \Le s \Le kh ,\ 0 \Le j \Le k-1 \bigr) , \qquad
	\mathbb E_k[\,\cdot\,] := \mathbb E\bigl[\,\cdot\, \big| \mathcal F_{k}\bigr] ,
	\label{eq: filtration}
\end{equation}
so that $\chi_k$ and $\xi_k$ are independent of $\mathcal F_{k}$. We construct a Markov chain $(Y_k)_{k\Ge0}$ by
\begin{equation}
	Y_{k+1} = Y_k - h\, \hat g\Big(Y_k + \sqrt{\frac h2}\, \chi_k; \xi_k\Big) + \sqrt{2h}\, \chi_k .
	\label{eq: sglm markov}
	\tag{SGLM'}
\end{equation}
Then $Z_k = Y_k + \sqrt{h/2}\, \chi_k$ satisfies \eqref{eq: sglm}, so the SGLM integrator is equivalent to \eqref{eq: sglm markov}. Here $Y_k$ is $\mathcal F_{k}$-measurable and $Z_k$ is $\mathcal F_{k+1}$-measurable. For the samples of \eqref{eq: sglm} from a given $Z_0$, the chain starts at $Y_1 = Z_0 - h\, \hat g(Z_0;\xi_0) + \sqrt{h/2}\, \chi_0$, and $Z_k = Y_k + \sqrt{h/2}\, \chi_k$ for $k \Ge 1$. With the full gradient, \eqref{eq: sglm markov} is the form in which LM is analyzed as a postprocessed integrator in \citep{vilmart2015postprocessed}.

Since $\chi_k$ is determined by the Brownian motion on $[kh,(k+1)h]$, we call \eqref{eq: sglm markov} the Markovian form of SGLM. The analysis through the discrete Poisson equation is carried out on the Markovian form $(Y_k)_{k \Ge 0}$, not on the original form $(Z_k)_{k \Ge 0}$, and their connection is made by the modified class of Section~\ref{sec: modified}. With the full gradient, the Markovian form has strong and weak order $1$ \citep{ye2026mse}. The second order discretization error of LM therefore comes entirely from the Gaussian shift $\sqrt{h/2}\, \chi_k$ between $Y_k$ and $Z_k$.

\subsection{Elliptic estimates of the discrete Poisson equation}
\label{subsec: poisson}

The generator of the overdamped Langevin dynamics \eqref{eq: langevin} is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L \psi = \Delta \psi - \nabla U \cdot \nabla \psi ,
    \label{eq: generator}
\end{equation}
and $e^{t\mathcal L}$, $t \Ge 0$, is its semigroup, $e^{t\mathcal L} f(x) = \mathbb E^x[f(X_t)]$, where $X_t$ solves \eqref{eq: langevin} with $X_0 = x$. The Dirichlet form of $\mathcal L$ \citep{bakry2014analysis} gives the integration by parts
\begin{equation}
    \pi(f_1\, \mathcal L f_2) = -\pi(\nabla f_1 \cdot \nabla f_2)
    \label{eq: generator ibp}
\end{equation}
for $f_1$ and $f_2$ whose derivatives of orders $0$ to $2$ grow at most polynomially.

For a Lipschitz test function $f$, the Kolmogorov solution $u(x,t) := e^{t\mathcal L} f(x) - \pi(f)$ solves the parabolic equation $\partial_t u = \mathcal L u$ with $u(\cdot,0) = f - \pi(f)$. By the exponential convergence of $u(x,t)$ established in Theorem~\ref{thm: u decay} below, the discrete Poisson solution \citep{lu2025mse, ye2026mse}
\begin{equation}
    \phi_h(x) := h \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} u(x,kh) = h\, \bigl( \mathcal I - e^{h\mathcal L} \bigr)^{-1} \bigl( f(x) - \pi(f) \bigr) ,
    \label{eq: discrete poisson solution}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal I$ the identity operator, is well defined and, by telescoping the series with $e^{h\mathcal L} u(\cdot, kh) = u(\cdot, (k+1)h)$ and the exponential decay \eqref{eq: u bound} of $u$, satisfies the discrete Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\phi_h(x) - (e^{h\mathcal L} \phi_h)(x)}{h} = f(x) - \pi(f) , \qquad x \in \mathbb R^d .
    \label{eq: discrete poisson}
\end{equation}
As $h \to 0$, \eqref{eq: discrete poisson solution} formally becomes the continuous Poisson solution
\begin{equation}
	\phi_0(x) = \int_0^\infty u(x,t)\, \D t = (-\mathcal L)^{-1}\bigl( f(x) - \pi(f) \bigr),
	\label{eq: continuous poisson solution}
\end{equation}
which solves the continuous Poisson equation \citep{mattingly2010convergence, vollmer2016exploration, brosse2019tamed}
\begin{equation}
    -\mathcal L \phi_0(x) = f(x) - \pi(f) , \qquad x \in \mathbb R^d .
    \label{eq: continuous poisson}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: u decay}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, there are $\lambda > 0$ and $C$, depending only on $m$, $M$, $R$ and $d$, such that for every Lipschitz function $f$, $x \in \mathbb R^d$ and $t > 0$, $u(\cdot,t) \in C^3(\mathbb R^d)$, $\nabla^3 u(\cdot,t)$ is Lipschitz, and
    \begin{equation}
        |u(x,t)| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty\, (1 + |x|)\, e^{-\lambda t} , \qquad
        \norm{\nabla^j u(\cdot,t)}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty \min(1,t)^{-\frac{j-1}{2}}\, e^{-\lambda t} , \quad j = 1, 2, 3, 4 .
        \label{eq: u bound}
    \end{equation}
    Consequently, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$, the discrete Poisson solution $\phi_h$ in \eqref{eq: discrete poisson solution} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    	\begin{gathered}
        \norm{\nabla ( e^{h\mathcal L} \phi_h )}_\infty + \norm{\nabla^2 ( e^{h\mathcal L} \phi_h )}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty ,\\
        \norm{\nabla^3 ( e^{h\mathcal L} \phi_h )}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty \log\frac1h , \qquad
        \norm{\nabla^4 ( e^{h\mathcal L} \phi_h )}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^{-\frac12} .
        \end{gathered}
        \label{eq: poisson bound}
    \end{equation}
    Moreover, the continuous Poisson solution $\phi_0$ in \eqref{eq: continuous poisson solution} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    	\norm{\nabla \phi_0}_\infty + \norm{\nabla^2 \phi_0}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty .
    	\label{eq: continuous poisson bound}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{app: elliptic}.)}
\end{theorem}

For $j \Ge 2$, the bounds \eqref{eq: u bound} inherit the singularity $t^{-\frac{j-1}{2}}$ of the heat semigroup. In the discrete Poisson solution, the term $h\, ( f - \pi(f) )$ is only Lipschitz, and the bounds \eqref{eq: poisson bound} of orders $3$ and $4$ come from the sums of $h\, (kh)^{-1}$ and of $h\, (kh)^{-\frac32}$ over the time steps $kh \Le 1$.

\begin{remark}
\label{rem: regular}
If $f \in C^3(\mathbb R^d)$ has bounded derivatives of orders $1$ to $3$, then the bound of order $3$ in \eqref{eq: poisson bound} holds without the factor $\log\frac1h$, with $\max_{1 \Le j \Le 3} \norm{\nabla^j f}_\infty$ in place of $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Riesz representors}
\label{subsec: riesz}

For a matrix-valued function $\mathcal A$ and $\phi_0$ of \eqref{eq: continuous poisson}, the expectation $\pi( \nabla^2 \phi_0 : \mathcal A )$ can be written as $\pi(f \psi_{\mathcal A})$, with a function $\psi_{\mathcal A}$ that does not depend on $f$. The construction of $\psi_{\mathcal A}$ uses the Poincar\'e inequality of $\pi$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: poincare}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, there is a constant $C_P$, depending only on $m$, $M$, $R$ and $d$, such that
    \begin{equation}
        \pi\bigl( (f - \pi(f))^2 \bigr) \Le C_P\, \pi\bigl( |\nabla f|^2 \bigr)
        \label{eq: poincare}
    \end{equation}
    for every $f \in C^1(\mathbb R^d)$ with a bounded gradient.\\[4pt]
    \emph{(Directly derived from \citep[Corollary~1.6 and Theorem~1.4]{bakry2008simple}; see Appendix~\ref{sec: riesz well-posedness} for further discussion.)}
\end{lemma}

For a bounded measurable function $\mathcal A : \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^{d \times d}$ such that $\mathcal A(x)$ is a symmetric matrix for every $x \in \mathbb R^d$, define the Riesz representor of $\mathcal A$ as the function $\psi_{\mathcal A} \in L^2(\pi)$ with $\pi(\psi_{\mathcal A}) = 0$ and
\begin{equation}
    \pi\bigl( \psi_{\mathcal A}(x)\, (-\mathcal L \omega)(x) \bigr) = \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \omega(x) : \mathcal A(x) \bigr)
    \label{eq: riesz}
\end{equation}
for every $\omega \in C^4(\mathbb R^d)$ with $\max_{1 \Le j \Le 4} \norm{\nabla^j \omega}_\infty < \infty$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: riesz}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, let $\mathcal A : \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^{d \times d}$ be bounded and measurable, with $\mathcal A(x)$ a symmetric matrix for every $x \in \mathbb R^d$. Then there is a unique $\psi_{\mathcal A} \in L^2(\pi)$ with $\pi(\psi_{\mathcal A}) = 0$ that satisfies \eqref{eq: riesz} for every $\omega \in C^4(\mathbb R^d)$ with $\max_{1 \Le j \Le 4} \norm{\nabla^j \omega}_\infty < \infty$, the map $\mathcal A \mapsto \psi_{\mathcal A}$ is linear, and
    \begin{equation}
        \pi\bigl( \psi_{\mathcal A}^2 \bigr) \Le d\, (1 + M C_P) \norm{\mathcal A}_\infty^2 ,
        \label{eq: riesz bound}
    \end{equation}
    with $C_P$ the constant of Lemma~\ref{lem: poincare}.
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: riesz well-posedness}.)}
\end{theorem}

For a Lipschitz $\mathcal A$, the representor has a gradient in $L^2(\pi)$, and \eqref{eq: riesz} extends to the solution $\phi_0$ of \eqref{eq: continuous poisson} for a Lipschitz test function.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: representor gradient}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, let $\mathcal A$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm: riesz} and Lipschitz. Then $\psi_{\mathcal A}$ has a weak gradient $\nabla \psi_{\mathcal A} \in L^2(\pi)$, and for every $\omega \in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ with $\norm{\nabla \omega}_\infty + \norm{\nabla^2 \omega}_\infty < \infty$,
    \begin{equation}
        \pi\bigl( \nabla \psi_{\mathcal A} \cdot \nabla \omega \bigr) = \pi\bigl( \psi_{\mathcal A}\, (-\mathcal L \omega) \bigr) = \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \omega : \mathcal A \bigr) , \qquad
        \pi\bigl( |\nabla \psi_{\mathcal A}|^2 \bigr) \Le \pi\bigl( |\mathcal A\, \nabla U - \operatorname{div} \mathcal A|^2 \bigr) ,
        \label{eq: representor gradient}
    \end{equation}
    with $(\operatorname{div} \mathcal A)_j = \sum_{i=1}^d \partial_i \mathcal A_{ij}$. In particular, for a Lipschitz function $f$,
    \begin{equation}
        \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0 : \mathcal A \bigr) = \pi( f \psi_{\mathcal A} ) .
        \label{eq: representor poisson}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: riesz well-posedness}.)}
\end{lemma}

\section{Modified class for SGLM}
\label{sec: modified}

The MSE \eqref{eq: mse} averages the test function along the samples $(Z_k)_{k \Ge 0}$ of the \eqref{eq: sglm} integrator, while the analysis runs on the Markovian form \eqref{eq: sglm markov}. The Gaussian shift between the two is absorbed by the \emph{modified class}: a modified test function $\tilde f_h$, a modified potential $\tilde U_h$ with its modified generator $\tilde{\mathcal L}_h$, and a modified discrete Poisson solution $\tilde\phi_h$. The modified class is local: its construction does not involve the long-time behavior. Section~\ref{subsec: modified test function} defines the modified test function $\tilde f_h$ as the heat flow of $f$, which splits the MSE into the time average error of the Markovian form and a bias against a reference measure. Section~\ref{subsec: modified poisson} defines the modified generator $\tilde{\mathcal L}_h$ and the modified discrete Poisson solution $\tilde\phi_h$. \subsection{Modified test function $\tilde f_h$}
\label{subsec: modified test function}

The positions $Z_k$ and $Y_k$ in \eqref{eq: sglm} and \eqref{eq: sglm markov} differ by the Gaussian shift $\sqrt{h/2}\, \chi_k \sim \mathcal N(0, \frac h2 I_d)$, which is independent of $\mathcal F_{k}$. The conditional mean of $f(Z_k)$ given $\mathcal F_{k}$ is therefore the heat semigroup applied to $f$ at time $\frac h4$, evaluated at $Y_k$. This motivates the definition of the \emph{modified test function}
\begin{equation}
	\tilde f_h(y) := (e^{\frac h4\Delta }f)(y) = 
	\mathbb E\biggl[ f\Bigl( y + \sqrt{\frac h2}\, \chi \Bigr) \biggr] , \quad \chi \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d) ,
	\label{eq: heat shift}
\end{equation}
for which $\mathbb E_k\bigl[ f(Z_k) \bigr] = \tilde f_h(Y_k)$. Since $\tilde f_h$ is an average of translates of $f$, it is Lipschitz with the constant $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$, and the Gaussian average makes it smooth. With $\mathcal N(0, \frac h2 I_d)$ also denoting the density, $\nabla^j \tilde f_h = (\nabla f) * \nabla^{j-1} \mathcal N(0, \frac h2 I_d)$ and $\int_{\mathbb R^d} \bigl\| \nabla^{j-1} \mathcal N(0, \frac h2 I_d)(y) \bigr\|\, \D y \Le C h^{-\frac{j-1}{2}}$, so that
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\nabla \tilde f_h}_\infty \Le \norm{\nabla f}_\infty , \qquad
    \norm{\nabla^j \tilde f_h}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^{-\frac{j-1}{2}} , \quad j = 2, 3, 4 .
    \label{eq: heat derivatives}
\end{equation}

The difference $\mathfrak M_k := f(Z_k) - \tilde f_h(Y_k)$ satisfies $\mathbb E_k[\mathfrak M_k] = 0$ by \eqref{eq: heat shift}, and 
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E_k\bigl[ \mathfrak M_k^2 \bigr] \Le \mathbb E_k\Bigl[ \bigl| f(Z_k) - f(Y_k) \bigr|^2 \Bigr] \Le \norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2\, \frac h2\, \mathbb E |\chi|^2 = \frac{d h}{2}\, \norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2 .
    \label{eq: martingale variance}
\end{equation}
The $\mathfrak M_k$ are $\mathcal F_{k+1}$-measurable martingale differences, so their sum has additive variance. For every probability measure $\pi_\star$ on $\mathbb R^d$ with a finite first moment, inserting $f(Z_k) = \tilde f_h(Y_k) + \mathfrak M_k$ into the MSE \eqref{eq: mse}, applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to the three resulting terms and then the additive variance with \eqref{eq: martingale variance} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \MSE(N,h)
    &= \mathbb E \Biggl[ \biggl| \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \bigl( \tilde f_h(Y_k) - \pi_\star(\tilde f_h) \bigr) + \bigl( \pi_\star(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f) \bigr) + \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \mathfrak M_k \biggr|^2 \Biggr] \\
    &\Le 3\, \mathbb E \Biggl[ \biggl| \underbrace{\frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \bigl( \tilde f_h(Y_k) - \pi_\star(\tilde f_h) \bigr)}_{\text{time average error}} \biggr|^2 \Biggr] + 3\, \bigl( \underbrace{\pi_\star(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f)}_{\text{modified bias}} \bigr)^2 + \frac{3 d h}{2 N}\, \norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2 .
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq: mse split}
\end{equation}
The term $\frac{3 d h}{2 N}\, \norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2$ of \eqref{eq: mse split} is absorbed into $\frac{1}{Nh}$, so it remains to estimate the time average error and the modified bias. The measure $\pi_\star$ is to be chosen so that the modified bias is of order $h^2$ and the discretization part of the time average error of order $h^2 \log\frac1h$.

The measure $\pi_\star$ cannot simply be $\pi$. Since the Gaussian density is symmetric, the heat semigroup moves from the test function to the density of $\pi$, and, for $\pi_\star = \pi$,
\begin{equation}
	\pi(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f) = \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \bigl( e^{\frac h4 \Delta} \pi - \pi \bigr)\, \D x = \frac h4 \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \Delta \pi\, \D x + \mathcal O\bigl(\norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^2\bigr) ,
	\label{eq: shift expansion}
\end{equation}
whose leading coefficient $\int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \Delta \pi\, \D x$ is in general nonzero.

\subsection{Modified potential $\tilde U_h$}
\label{subsec: modified}

We aim to find a distribution $\pi_\star$ such that the modified bias $\pi_\star(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f)$ is of order $h^2$. In view of \eqref{eq: shift expansion}, we consider the ansatz
\begin{equation}
	\pi_\star \propto \exp\Bigl( - U - \frac h4\, V \Bigr) ,
	\label{eq: ansatz}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is a function to be determined and the coefficient $\frac h4$ matches that of \eqref{eq: shift expansion}. We derive $V = |\nabla U|^2 - \Delta U$ by a perturbation analysis. The perturbed potential $U + \frac h4 V$, once made rigorous by a cutoff far from the origin, is the \emph{modified potential} $\tilde U_h$, and its Boltzmann distribution $\tilde\pi_h \propto \exp(-\tilde U_h)$ is the \emph{modified target}.

\subsubsection*{Derivation of $V$ by perturbation}

For a test function $\psi$, the ansatz \eqref{eq: ansatz} gives
\begin{equation*}
\pi_\star(\psi) = \frac{\pi( \psi e^{-\frac h4 V} ) }{ \pi( e^{-\frac h4 V})} = \frac{\int_{\mathbb R^d} \psi e^{-U-\frac h4V}\D x}{\int_{\mathbb R^d} e^{-U-\frac h4V}\D x}.
\end{equation*}
Expanding $e^{-\frac h4 V} = 1 - \frac h4 V + \mathcal O(h^2)$ in the numerator and in the denominator of $\pi_\star(\psi)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
    \pi_\star(\psi) = \pi(\psi) - \frac h4 \bigl( \pi(\psi V) - \pi(\psi)\, \pi(V) \bigr) + \mathcal O(h^2) .
    \label{eq: rho expansion}
\end{equation}
Taking $\psi = \tilde f_h$ in \eqref{eq: rho expansion} and inserting \eqref{eq: shift expansion} gives
\begin{equation}
    \pi_\star(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f) = \frac h4 \Bigl( \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \Delta \pi\, \D x - \pi(f V) + \pi(f)\, \pi(V) \Bigr) + \mathcal O( h^2) .
    \label{eq: bias order h}
\end{equation}
The modified bias is therefore of order $h^2$ exactly when the term of order $h$ in \eqref{eq: bias order h} vanishes,
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \Delta \pi\, \D x = \pi(f V) - \pi(f)\, \pi(V) .
    \label{eq: V condition}
\end{equation}
Differentiating $\pi \propto e^{-U}$ to the order $2$ gives $\Delta \pi = ( |\nabla U|^2 - \Delta U )\, \pi$, so that
\begin{equation*}
    \int_{\mathbb R^d} f\, \Delta \pi\, \D x = \pi\bigl( f\, |\nabla U|^2 - f\, \Delta U \bigr) .
\end{equation*}
Hence we select the function
\begin{equation}
    V := |\nabla U|^2 - \Delta U = -\mathcal L U ,
    \label{eq: V}
\end{equation}
for which $\pi(V) = -\pi(\mathcal L U) = 0$ by the invariance of $\pi$.
This computation is formal, and the cutoff introduced below changes the measure \eqref{eq: ansatz}; Lemma~\ref{lem: shifted bias} proves the bias estimate for the modified target.

\subsubsection*{Cutoff in the modified potential}

The naive construction $U + \frac h4 V$ of the modified potential does not satisfy Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, because the derivatives of $|\nabla U|^2$ grow with $|x|$. Since Theorem~\ref{thm: u decay} must be applied to the modified potential with constants independent of $h$, the modification is cut off far from the origin. Fix a smooth cutoff function $\theta \in C^\infty(\mathbb R^d)$ with $0 \Le \theta \Le 1$, $\theta = 1$ on $\{|x| \Le 1\}$ and $\theta = 0$ on $\{|x| \Ge 2\}$. With $\delta \in (0,1]$ fixed in Lemma~\ref{lem: modified class} below, we define the modified potential by
\begin{equation}
    \tilde U_h := U + \frac h4\, V\, \theta_h , \qquad \theta_h(x) := \theta\Bigl( \frac{h x}{\delta} \Bigr) ,
    \label{eq: modified potential}
\end{equation}
so that $\tilde U_h = U + \frac h4 V$ on the ball $\{|x| \Le \delta/h\}$ and $\tilde U_h = U$ on $\{|x| \Ge 2\delta/h\}$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: modified class}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, there are $\delta \in (0,1]$ and $h_0 \in (0, \delta]$ such that for every $h \in (0,h_0]$, the modified potential $\tilde U_h \in C^5(\mathbb R^d)$ satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        |\nabla \tilde U_h(0)| \Le 2M , \qquad \max_{2 \Le i \Le 5}\, \norm{\nabla^i \tilde U_h(x)} \Le 2M , \qquad \forall x \in \mathbb R^d ,
        \label{eq: modified class bounds}
    \end{equation}
    the convexity condition $\nabla^2 \tilde U_h(x) \succeq \frac m2 I_d$ for $|x| \Ge R$, and
    \begin{equation}
        \max_{1 \Le i \Le 5}\, \norm{\nabla^i \tilde U_h(x) - \nabla^i U(x)} \Le C h \bigl( 1 + |x| \bigr) , \qquad \forall x \in \mathbb R^d .
        \label{eq: modified class}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: modified class proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

In the rest of the paper, the number $\delta$ is fixed by Lemma~\ref{lem: modified class} and absorbed into the constant $C$.

\subsection{Estimation of the modified bias $\tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f)$}
\label{subsec: modified bias}

The cancellation \eqref{eq: V condition} between the heat flow of the test function and the modification of the potential holds for the modified target $\tilde\pi_h$ as it does for the ansatz \eqref{eq: ansatz}, since the cutoff acts only where $\pi$ has exponentially small mass. Lemma~\ref{lem: shifted bias} carries the perturbation argument that produced $V$ over to the modified target.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: shifted bias}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$ and Lipschitz function $f$,
    \begin{equation}
        \bigl| \tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) - \pi(f) \bigr| \Le C h^2 \norm{\nabla f}_\infty .
        \label{eq: shifted bias}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: shifted bias proof}.)}
\end{lemma}
By the Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality, \eqref{eq: shifted bias} is equivalent to $\mathcal W_1\bigl( \tilde\pi_h * \mathcal N(0, \frac h2 I_d), \pi \bigr) \Le C h^2$.

With $\pi_\star = \tilde\pi_h$ in \eqref{eq: mse split}, Lemma~\ref{lem: shifted bias} bounds the modified bias by $C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^2$, and the MSE satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \MSE(N,h) \Le 3\, \mathbb E \Biggl[ \biggl| \underbrace{\frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \bigl( \tilde f_h(Y_k) - \tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) \bigr)}_{\text{time average error}} \biggr|^2 \Biggr] + C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2 \Big(h^4 + \frac{dh}{N}\Big).
    \label{eq: mse split modified}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Modified discrete Poisson solution $\tilde\phi_h$}
\label{subsec: modified poisson}

Let the \emph{modified dynamics} be the overdamped Langevin dynamics driven by the potential $\tilde U_h$,
\begin{equation}
    \D \tilde X_t = -\nabla \tilde U_h(\tilde X_t)\,\D t + \sqrt2\,\D B_t ,
    \label{eq: modified langevin}
\end{equation}
with the \emph{modified generator}
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathcal L}_h \psi = \Delta \psi - \nabla \tilde U_h \cdot \nabla \psi ,
    \label{eq: modified generator}
\end{equation}
and its semigroup $e^{t\tilde{\mathcal L}_h}$. The invariant distribution of \eqref{eq: modified langevin} is the modified target $\tilde\pi_h$. The time average error in \eqref{eq: mse split modified} is analyzed through the discrete Poisson equation of the modified generator \eqref{eq: modified generator}. Let $\tilde\phi_h$ be the discrete Poisson solution \eqref{eq: discrete poisson solution} with $\tilde{\mathcal L}_h$, $\tilde\pi_h$ and $\tilde f_h$ in place of $\mathcal L$, $\pi$ and $f$, so that
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\tilde\phi_h(x) - (e^{h\tilde{\mathcal L}_h} \tilde\phi_h)(x)}{h} = \tilde f_h(x) - \tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) , \qquad x \in \mathbb R^d .
    \label{eq: modified poisson}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor: modified poisson}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$ and Lipschitz function $f$, $\tilde\phi_h \in C^4(\mathbb R^d)$ and
    \begin{equation}
        \norm{\nabla \tilde\phi_h}_\infty + \norm{\nabla^2 \tilde\phi_h}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty  , \quad
        \norm{\nabla^3 \tilde\phi_h}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty \log\frac1h , \quad
        \norm{\nabla^4 \tilde\phi_h}_\infty \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^{-\frac12} .
        \label{eq: modified poisson bound}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: modified poisson proof}.)}
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor: modified poisson} is the counterpart of \eqref{eq: poisson bound} for the modified class, with \eqref{eq: heat derivatives} for the term $h\, ( \tilde f_h - \tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) )$ of $\tilde\phi_h$. Compared to $\phi_h$, the derivatives of $\tilde\phi_h$ can be directly controlled by $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$ because of the smoothing of the heat semigroup. As in Remark~\ref{rem: regular}, if $f$ has bounded derivatives of orders $1$ to $3$, the bound of order $3$ in \eqref{eq: modified poisson bound} holds without $\log\frac1h$, with $\max_{1 \Le j \Le 3} \norm{\nabla^j f}_\infty$ in place of $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$.

\section{Local error of SGLM}
\label{sec: local}

This section compares one step of the Markovian form \eqref{eq: sglm markov} with one step of the modified dynamics \eqref{eq: modified langevin}, and derives the local weak error used in the MSE analysis. Section~\ref{subsec: bias cutoff} bounds the moments of the chain uniformly in time and the sizes of the full gradient part of a step, and matches the moments of orders $1$ to $3$ of this part and of a step of the modified dynamics, with a mismatch of order $h^3$. Section~\ref{subsec: weak expansion} turns the matching into the local weak error of the SGLM integrator, in which the gradient noise adds an explicit term of order $h^2$ and a remainder of order $h^3$.

The discrete Poisson solution $\tilde\phi_h$ of \eqref{eq: modified poisson} transfers local errors into the time average error of \eqref{eq: mse split modified} \citep{lu2025mse, ye2026mse}. The local error studied in this section is therefore the error of one step of the Markovian form \eqref{eq: sglm markov} against one step of the modified dynamics from the same state.

Since the chain \eqref{eq: sglm markov} is time-homogeneous, statements about a single step are made at the index $0$ and apply at any $k$; Lemmas~\ref{lem: moments} and \ref{lem: sg weak} are stated at the index $k$. One step of the chain is
\begin{equation}
    Y_1 - Y_0 = \sqrt{2h}\, \chi_0 - h\, \hat g(Z_0;\xi_0) , \qquad Z_0 = Y_0 + \sqrt{\frac h2}\, \chi_0 ,
    \label{eq: sg chain step}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_0 \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$. For each $k \Ge 0$, let $(\tilde Y_k(t))_{t \Ge 0}$ solve the modified dynamics \eqref{eq: modified langevin} from $\tilde Y_k(0) = Y_k$, driven by $(B_{kh+t} - B_{kh})_{t \Ge 0}$ with the increment $\sqrt h\, \chi_k$. Then $\mathbb E_k[\psi(\tilde Y_k(h))] = (e^{h\tilde{\mathcal L}_h} \psi)(Y_k)$, and the local error of one step against the modified dynamics is $Y_1 - \tilde Y_0(h)$. With the full gradient step
\begin{equation}
    D_k := \sqrt{2h}\, \chi_k - h\, \nabla U(Z_k) ,
    \label{eq: full gradient step}
\end{equation}
the local error splits into a discretization part and the gradient noise at $Z_0$,
\begin{equation}
    Y_1 - \tilde Y_0(h) = \bigl( Y_0 + D_0 - \tilde Y_0(h) \bigr) - h\, \bigl( \hat g(Z_0;\xi_0) - \nabla U(Z_0) \bigr) .
    \label{eq: local error split}
\end{equation}
The discretization part $Y_0 + D_0 - \tilde Y_0(h)$ does not involve the stochastic gradient.

\subsection{Moment bounds and moment matching}
\label{subsec: bias cutoff}

The MSE analysis squares the polynomial weight of the local weak errors of Section~\ref{subsec: weak expansion}, and therefore needs moments of order $12$ of the chain, uniformly in time. The following lemma provides them. Its proof is standard and follows the Lyapunov function arguments in \citep{ye2026mse}, with Lemma~\ref{lem: modified class} for the modified dynamics.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: moments}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for the chain \eqref{eq: sglm markov} from a given $Y_0 \in \mathbb R^d$, every $h \in (0,h_0]$ and $k \Ge 0$,
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbb E |Y_k|^{12} \Le C \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr)^{12} , \qquad
        \max\bigl\{ \mathbb E |\tilde Y_k(h) - Y_k|^{12} ,\; \mathbb E |Y_{k+1} - Y_k|^{12} \bigr\} \Le C h^{6} \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr)^{12} .
        \label{eq: moment bounds}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: moments proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

The next lemma bounds the size of the full gradient step $D_0$ and of one step of the modified dynamics, and the size of the discretization part of the local error \eqref{eq: local error split}.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: sizes}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, for the full gradient step $D_0$ of \eqref{eq: full gradient step} and every $s \in [0,h]$,
    \begin{equation}
        \pnorm{D_0}_4 \Le C \sqrt h \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr) , \quad
        \pnorm{\tilde Y_0(s) - Y_0}_4 \Le C \sqrt h \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr) , \quad
        \pnorm{Y_0 + D_0 - \tilde Y_0(h)}_4 \Le C h^{\frac32} \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr) ,
        \label{eq: sizes}
    \end{equation}
    where $\pnorm{\cdot}_4$ is taken under $\mathbb E_0$. Moreover, the discretization part of the local error has the expansion
    \begin{equation}
        \biggl\| Y_0 + D_0 - \tilde Y_0(h) - \sqrt2\, \nabla^2 U(Y_0) \int_0^h \Bigl( B_s - \frac12 B_h \Bigr) \D s \biggr\|_4 \Le C h^2 \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr)^2 ,
        \label{eq: local error expansion}
    \end{equation}
    where the Gaussian vector $\int_0^h ( B_s - \frac12 B_h )\, \D s$ is centered and independent of $\chi_0$. \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: moments proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

Compared with the exact dynamics \eqref{eq: langevin}, the full gradient step matches the moments of orders $2$ and $3$ to $\mathcal O(h^3)$ but misses the mean by $\frac{h^2}{4}\nabla V$, with $V$ as in \eqref{eq: V}; the drift correction of the modified dynamics removes exactly this mismatch. The cutoff keeps the cancellation on the ball $\{|Y_0| \Le \delta/h\}$, and outside the ball the remaining mismatch is again of order $h^3$, because $h|Y_0|/\delta \Ge 1$ there.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: moment matching}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U}, for the full gradient step $D_0$ of \eqref{eq: full gradient step},
    \begin{equation}
        \Bigl\| \mathbb E_0\bigl[ D_0^{\otimes j} \bigr] - \mathbb E_0\bigl[ (\tilde Y_0(h) - Y_0)^{\otimes j} \bigr] \Bigr\| \Le C h^3 \bigl( 1 + |Y_0| \bigr)^{6} , \qquad j = 1, 2, 3 .
        \label{eq: moment matching}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: matching proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Local weak error expansion}
\label{subsec: weak expansion}

The local weak error of SGLM on a test function $G$ is $\mathbb E_0\bigl[ G(Y_1) - G(\tilde Y_0(h)) \bigr]$, and the MSE analysis takes $G = \tilde\phi_h$. By \eqref{eq: local error split}, it has a part from the discretization and a part from the gradient noise. For the discretization part, because $Y_0 + D_0 - \tilde Y_0(h)$ is of size $h^{\frac32}$, against $h^2$ for the SRK integrators of \citep{ye2026mse}, $G$ is expanded along a step to the order $3$: the terms of orders $1$ to $3$ are matched by Lemma~\ref{lem: moment matching}, and the remainder is bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem: sizes}. The remainder carries the derivative of order $4$ of $G$, which for $G = \tilde\phi_h$ is of size $h^{-\frac12}$ by \eqref{eq: modified poisson bound}. The size of the remainder is $h^3 \norm{\nabla^4 G}_\infty$, but its mean is of order $h^{\frac72} \norm{\nabla^4 G}_\infty$, since the part of size $h^{\frac32}$ of $Y_0 + D_0 - \tilde Y_0(h)$ is centered and independent of $\chi_0$ by \eqref{eq: local error expansion}.

For the part from the gradient noise, the gradient noise at $x$ has the covariance
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal C(x) := \mathbb E_\xi\Bigl[ \bigl( \hat g(x;\xi) - \nabla U(x) \bigr) \otimes \bigl( \hat g(x;\xi) - \nabla U(x) \bigr) \Bigr] , \qquad \mathrm{Tr}\bigl[ \mathcal C(x) \bigr] \Le \Sigma ,
    \label{eq: covariance}
\end{equation}
where the bound on the trace holds by Assumption~\ref{ass: sg}. The covariance $\mathcal C$ is Lipschitz (Appendix~\ref{sec: sg weak proof}),
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\mathcal C(x) - \mathcal C(x')} \Le 4 M^2 |x - x'| , \qquad x, x' \in \mathbb R^d .
    \label{eq: covariance lipschitz}
\end{equation}
Given $\mathcal F_0$ and $\chi_0$, the gradient noise at $Z_0$ in \eqref{eq: local error split} has mean $0$, so it does not change the mean of the step, while the moment of order $2$ of the step gains $h^2\, \mathbb E_0[\mathcal C(Z_0)]$.

Lemma~\ref{lem: sg weak} keeps this gain as an explicit term of order $h^2$, bounded through \eqref{eq: covariance} by $\frac{\Sigma h^2}{2}\, \norm{\nabla^2 G}_\infty$, while the rest of the gradient noise contributes only a remainder of order $h^3$, the order of the terms matched in the discretization part. In the explicit term, the Hessian of $G$ is evaluated at $Y_k + D_k$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: sg weak}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for every $G \in C^4(\mathbb R^d)$ with $\max_{1 \Le j \Le 4} \norm{\nabla^j G}_\infty < \infty$ and $k \Ge 0$, the chain \eqref{eq: sglm markov} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        \biggl| \mathbb E_k \bigl[ G(Y_{k+1}) - G(\tilde Y_k(h)) \bigr] - \frac{h^2}{2}\, \mathbb E_k\bigl[ \nabla^2 G(Y_k + D_k) : \mathcal C(Z_k) \bigr] \biggr|
        \Le C \Bigl( h^3 \sum_{j=1}^{3} \norm{\nabla^j G}_\infty + h^{\frac72} \norm{\nabla^4 G}_\infty \Bigr) \bigl( 1 + |Y_k| \bigr)^{6} .
    \label{eq: sg weak}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: sg weak proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

\section{Main results on SGLM}
\label{sec: mse}

This section states the main results: the MSE bound for \eqref{eq: sglm} in Section~\ref{subsec: mse analysis}, the numerical bias of SGLM in Section~\ref{subsec: numerical bias}, the MSE bound and the numerical bias of \eqref{eq: sgld} in Section~\ref{subsec: sgld}, and the explicit coefficients of $h$ in $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)$ and $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)$, given by the Riesz representors, with their comparison over the batch size, in Section~\ref{subsec: w1}. Their proofs are in Appendices~\ref{app: mse}, \ref{app: bias}, \ref{app: sgld} and \ref{app: w1}.

\subsection{MSE analysis of SGLM}
\label{subsec: mse analysis}

With $\pi_\star = \tilde\pi_h$, the MSE satisfies \eqref{eq: mse split modified}, and it remains to bound its time average error. Telescoping $\tilde\phi_h(Y_{k+1}) - \tilde\phi_h(Y_k)$ with the solution $\tilde\phi_h$ of \eqref{eq: modified poisson} gives the time-averaged error formula of \citep{lu2025mse, ye2026mse},
\begin{equation}
    \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \bigl( \tilde f_h(Y_k) - \tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) \bigr)
    = \frac{\tilde\phi_h(Y_0) - \tilde\phi_h(Y_N)}{Nh} + \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \Upsilon_k + \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \Psi_k ,
    \label{eq: time average formula}
\end{equation}
with the discretization term and the martingale difference
\begin{equation}
    \Upsilon_k := \frac{\tilde\phi_h(Y_{k+1}) - \tilde\phi_h(\tilde Y_k(h))}{h} , \qquad
    \Psi_k := \frac{\tilde\phi_h(\tilde Y_k(h)) - \tilde\phi_h(Y_k)}{h} + \tilde f_h(Y_k) - \tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h) .
    \label{eq: Upsilon Psi}
\end{equation}
The term $\Upsilon_k$ is bounded using Lemma~\ref{lem: sg weak}, and $\Psi_k$ has conditional mean $0$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: sglm mse}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, let $f$ be Lipschitz and let $(Z_k)_{k \Ge 0}$ be the samples of \eqref{eq: sglm} from a given $Z_0 \in \mathbb R^d$. Then, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$ and $N \Ge 1$,
    \begin{equation}
        \MSE(N,h) \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty^2 \biggl[ \bigl( 1 + |Z_0| \bigr)^2 \Bigl( \frac{1}{Nh} + \Sigma^2 h^2 \Bigr) + \bigl( 1 + |Z_0| \bigr)^{12} h^4 \log^2\frac1h \biggr] .
        \label{eq: sglm mse}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{app: mse}.)}
\end{theorem}

The term $\frac{1}{Nh}$ is the order of the variance of a time average over a trajectory of length $Nh$. The explicit term of Lemma~\ref{lem: sg weak}, divided by $h$, adds to the conditional mean of $\Upsilon_k$ a term of order $\Sigma h$, whose square gives the term $\Sigma^2 h^2$. The term $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ is the square of the discretization error, of order $h^2 \log\frac1h$, which comes from Lemma~\ref{lem: shifted bias} and the right side of \eqref{eq: sg weak}. The logarithm is that of the bound of order $3$ in \eqref{eq: modified poisson bound}, and is removed if $f$ has bounded derivatives of orders $1$ to $3$.

For the full gradient $\hat g(x;\xi) = \nabla U(x)$, Assumption~\ref{ass: sg} holds with $\Sigma = 0$, and \eqref{eq: sglm mse} gives the MSE bound of \eqref{eq: lm}, with the terms $\frac{1}{Nh}$ and $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ only. As $N \to \infty$, only $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ remains, so the numerical bias of \eqref{eq: lm} is of order $h^2 \log\frac1h$ for every Lipschitz test function.

With a batch of $P$ independent stochastic gradients, the \eqref{eq: sglm} integrator reads
\begin{equation*}
    Z_{k+1} = Z_k - \frac hP \sum_{p=1}^{P} \hat g\bigl(Z_k; \xi_k^{(p)}\bigr) + \sqrt{\frac h2}\, \bigl( \chi_k + \chi_{k+1} \bigr) ,
\end{equation*}
with $\xi_k^{(1)}, \dots, \xi_k^{(P)}$ independent and drawn afresh at every step. By Minkowski's inequality, the averaged estimator keeps the bound $M(1 + |x|)$ on its $L^{12}$ norm and the bound on its derivative, uniformly in $P$, and its variance is at most $\Sigma/P$, so it satisfies Assumption~\ref{ass: sg} with $\Sigma/P$ in place of $\Sigma$; the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm: sglm mse} is unchanged, and \eqref{eq: sglm mse} holds with $\Sigma^2 h^2 / P^2$ in place of $\Sigma^2 h^2$. 

\subsection{Numerical bias estimate of SGLM}
\label{subsec: numerical bias}

The MSE bound controls the time average along one trajectory. This subsection identifies the long-time limit of that average for \eqref{eq: sglm}, and its difference from $\pi(f)$, the numerical bias.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: invariant}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$, \eqref{eq: sglm markov} has a unique invariant distribution $\mu_h$, with $\mu_h(|y|^{12}) \Le C$. Let $\pi_h := \mu_h * \mathcal N(0, \frac h2 I_d)$. Then, for every Lipschitz function $f$ and given $Z_0 \in \mathbb R^d$, the samples of \eqref{eq: sglm} satisfy
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} f(Z_k) = \pi_h(f) \qquad \text{almost surely} .
        \label{eq: ergodic limit}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: invariant proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

Since \eqref{eq: sglm} is not Markovian, it has no invariant distribution in the usual sense; by \eqref{eq: ergodic limit}, we call $\pi_h$ the numerical invariant distribution of SGLM.

The next theorem expands the numerical bias $\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)$ to the order $h$, with a coefficient given by the solution $\phi_0$ of the continuous Poisson equation \eqref{eq: continuous poisson} and the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$ of the gradient noise in \eqref{eq: covariance}.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: numerical bias}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$ and Lipschitz function $f$, the numerical invariant distribution $\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sglm} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        \Bigl| \pi_h(f) - \pi(f) - \frac h2\, \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \mathcal C(x) \bigr) \Bigr| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^2 \log\frac1h .
        \label{eq: numerical bias}
    \end{equation}
    In particular, $|\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty ( \Sigma h + h^2 \log\frac1h )$. \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: numerical bias proof}.)}
\end{theorem}

The bound $C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty (\Sigma h + h^2 \log\frac1h)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm: numerical bias} follows from \eqref{eq: continuous poisson bound} and $\norm{\mathcal C(x)} \Le \mathrm{Tr}\bigl[ \mathcal C(x) \bigr] \Le \Sigma$ by \eqref{eq: covariance}. In the proof, the modified discrete Poisson equation \eqref{eq: modified poisson} under the invariant distribution $\mu_h$ of Lemma~\ref{lem: invariant}, with Lemma~\ref{lem: sg weak}, gives the coefficient as an expectation of $\nabla^2 \tilde\phi_h : \mathcal C$ under $\mu_h$. The bound $\mathcal W_1(\mu_h, \pi) \Le C h$ moves this expectation to $\pi$, and the Riesz representor $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ identifies it with $\pi( \nabla^2 \phi_0 : \mathcal C )$.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor: bias coefficient}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for every Lipschitz function $f$, the numerical invariant distribution $\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sglm} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)}{h} = \frac12\, \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \mathcal C(x) \bigr) .
        \label{eq: bias coefficient}
    \end{equation}
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm: numerical bias} gives a rigorous characterization of the linear part of the numerical bias of \eqref{eq: sglm}: its coefficient comes from the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$ of the stochastic gradient, and vanishes with the full gradient.

Linear response theory \citep{kubo1957statistical, hairer2010simple} also explains the coefficient \eqref{eq: bias coefficient}. To first order in $h$, the gradient noise of \eqref{eq: sglm} adds the diffusion $\frac h2\, \mathcal C : \nabla^2$ to the generator \eqref{eq: generator}, and the invariant distribution responds to this perturbation by $\frac h2\, \pi(\nabla^2 \phi_0 : \mathcal C)$ for the test function $f$. The invariant distributions of Langevin integrators are viewed in the same way, as perturbations of $\pi$ depending on the step size, in \citep{leimkuhler2015computation}. With the solution of the continuous Poisson equation \eqref{eq: continuous poisson} written as $\phi_0 = \int_0^\infty e^{t\mathcal L} \bigl( f - \pi(f) \bigr) \D t$, two integrations by parts give the Green--Kubo form \citep{green1954markoff, kubo1957statistical} of the coefficient,
\begin{equation*}
    \frac12\, \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0 : \mathcal C \bigr) = \frac12 \int_0^\infty \pi\Bigl( \pi^{-1}
     \nabla^2 : (\mathcal C \pi)\; e^{t\mathcal L} \bigl( f - \pi(f) \bigr) \Bigr) \D t , \qquad \nabla^2 : (\mathcal C \pi) = \sum_{i,j=1}^d \partial_i \partial_j \bigl( \mathcal C_{ij}\, \pi \bigr) ,
\end{equation*}
that is, the time integral of the correlation between $\pi^{-1} \nabla^2 : (\mathcal C \pi)$ at the time $0$ and $f$ at the time $t$, along \eqref{eq: langevin} started from $\pi$.

\subsection{Numerical bias comparison with SGLD}
\label{subsec: sgld}

The MSE analysis through the discrete Poisson equation applies to \eqref{eq: sgld} without extra effort: the samples of SGLD form a Markov chain, so no modified class is needed, and the time-averaged error formula \eqref{eq: time average formula} holds with $Z_k$, $f$, $\pi(f)$, the discrete Poisson solution $\phi_h$ of \eqref{eq: discrete poisson solution} and the solution $X_h$ of \eqref{eq: langevin} from $X_0 = Z_k$ in place of $Y_k$, $\tilde f_h$, $\tilde\pi_h(\tilde f_h)$, $\tilde\phi_h$ and $\tilde Y_k(h)$. For the samples of SGLD from a given $Z_0 \in \mathbb R^d$, this formula gives
\begin{equation}
    \MSE(N,h) = \mathcal O\Bigl( \frac{1}{Nh} + \Sigma^2 h^2 + h^2 \Bigr) ,
    \label{eq: sgld mse}
\end{equation}
with the constant depending also on $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$ and $Z_0$. The conditional mean of the discretization term $\bigl( \phi_h(Z_{k+1}) - \phi_h(X_h) \bigr)/h$ is bounded by Lemmas~\ref{lem: sgld weak} and \ref{lem: sgld lipschitz weak} in Appendix~\ref{app: sgld}, and the other terms are bounded as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm: sglm mse}. The term $\Sigma^2 h^2$ comes from the gradient noise, as in \eqref{eq: sglm mse}, and the last term $h^2$ from the discretization error of EM, where \eqref{eq: sglm mse} has $h^4 \log^2\frac1h$. Since $\Sigma \Le M^2$, \eqref{eq: sgld mse} reduces to $\MSE(N,h) = \mathcal O\bigl( (Nh)^{-1} + h^2 \bigr)$, the bound proved through the continuous Poisson equation \eqref{eq: continuous poisson} in \citep[Theorem~9]{vollmer2016exploration} and \citep[Theorem~3]{chen2015convergence} when the derivatives of $\phi_0$ are bounded.

The arguments of Lemma~\ref{lem: invariant}, based on Harris's ergodic theorem, show that \eqref{eq: sgld} has a unique invariant distribution $\bar\pi_h$, with $\bar\pi_h(|x|^{12}) \Le C$, and that the time averages of $f$ along SGLD converge almost surely to $\bar\pi_h(f)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem: sgld invariant} in Appendix~\ref{app: sgld}). The invariant distribution $\bar\pi_h$ differs from the numerical invariant distribution $\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sglm} in the coefficient of order $h$ of the numerical bias. In the next theorem, this coefficient carries, besides the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$ of the gradient noise, the one-step error of EM against \eqref{eq: langevin}, through $\nabla^2 U\, \nabla U - \nabla \Delta U$ in the mean of the step and the Hessian $\nabla^2 U$ in its moment of order $2$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: sgld bias}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$ and Lipschitz function $f$, the invariant distribution $\bar\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sgld} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        & \biggl| \bar\pi_h(f) - \pi(f) - \frac h2\, \bar\pi_h\Bigl( \nabla^2 ( e^{h\Delta} \phi_h ) : \bigl( 2\nabla^2 U + \mathcal C \bigr) \Bigr) \\
        & \quad + \frac h2\, \bar\pi_h\Bigl( \nabla ( e^{h\Delta} \phi_h ) \cdot \bigl( \nabla^2 U\, \nabla U - \nabla \Delta U \bigr) \Bigr) \biggr| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^2 \log\frac1h ,
    \end{aligned}
        \label{eq: sgld bias}
    \end{equation}
    with $e^{h\Delta}$ the heat semigroup of Section~\ref{subsec: notation}, and
    \begin{equation}
        \Bigl| \bar\pi_h(f) - \pi(f) - \frac h2\, \pi\Bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \bigl( \nabla^2 U(x) + \mathcal C(x) \bigr) \Bigr) \Bigr| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h^{\frac{21}{11}} .
        \label{eq: sgld bias continuous}
    \end{equation}
    In particular, $|\bar\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty h$. \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: sgld bias proof}.)}
\end{theorem}

In \eqref{eq: sgld bias}, the expectations are taken under $\bar\pi_h$, and the heat semigroup $e^{h\Delta}$ of the Gaussian increment of the step of \eqref{eq: sgld} smooths the term $h\, ( f - \pi(f) )$ of $\phi_h$, which is only Lipschitz. In \eqref{eq: sgld bias continuous}, the expectations are taken under $\pi$, and only the Hessian $\nabla^2 \phi_0$ remains.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor: sgld coefficient}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, for every Lipschitz function $f$, the invariant distribution $\bar\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sgld} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\bar\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)}{h} = \frac12\, \pi\Bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \bigl( \nabla^2 U(x) + \mathcal C(x) \bigr) \Bigr) .
        \label{eq: sgld coefficient}
    \end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The coefficient \eqref{eq: sgld coefficient} is not entirely new: the coefficient of order $h$ of the numerical bias of \eqref{eq: sgld} has been studied formally in \citep[Section~4.1]{vollmer2016exploration}, for $d = 1$ and under the assumption that the chain is ergodic. There the gradient noise adds to the coefficient of EM a term in the variance of the stochastic gradient, and the modified SGLD of \citep[Section~4.2]{vollmer2016exploration} removes this term. Theorem~\ref{thm: sgld bias} proves the limit in $\mathbb R^d$, with a rate, and writes the coefficient in terms of the solution $\phi_0$ of \eqref{eq: continuous poisson}.

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm: sgld bias} also requires the stochastic gradient to have a finite moment of high order, as in Assumption~\ref{ass: sg}. For \eqref{eq: sgld} with a strongly convex potential, \citep[Theorem~1]{paulin2026sgld} bounds the numerical bias in $\mathcal W_2$ by a term of order $h$ if the moment of order $4$ of the gradient noise under $\pi$ is finite; if only the moment of order $2$ is finite, the bound could deteriorate to the order $h^{\frac12}$.
\end{remark}

The coefficients \eqref{eq: bias coefficient} of \eqref{eq: sglm} and \eqref{eq: sgld coefficient} of \eqref{eq: sgld} differ by $\frac12\, \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \phi_0(x) : \nabla^2 U(x) \bigr)$, the contribution of the discretization error of EM. This term does not depend on the gradient noise: it is the coefficient of SGLD with the full gradient, and it remains at every batch size. The coefficient of SGLM, by contrast, vanishes with $\mathcal C(x)$, and with a batch of $P$ independent stochastic gradients, whose average has the covariance $\mathcal C(x)/P$, it is divided by $P$. For a given test function, the two coefficients are not ordered in absolute value, since $\nabla^2 \phi_0$ is indefinite in general and the terms in $\nabla^2 U(x)$ and in $\mathcal C(x)$ can have opposite signs. Section~\ref{subsec: w1} compares the suprema of their absolute values over the test functions with $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty \Le 1$.

For every $\psi \in C^3(\mathbb R^d)$ with $\max_{1 \Le j \Le 3} \norm{\nabla^j \psi}_\infty < \infty$, \eqref{eq: generator} and \eqref{eq: generator ibp} with $f_1 = \partial_i \psi$ and $f_2 = \partial_i U$, summed over $i$, give
\begin{equation}
    \pi\Bigl( \nabla \psi(x) \cdot \bigl( \nabla^2 U(x)\, \nabla U(x) - \nabla \Delta U(x) \bigr) \Bigr) = \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \psi(x) : \nabla^2 U(x) \bigr) .
    \label{eq: sgld integration by parts}
\end{equation}
Under $\pi$, \eqref{eq: sgld integration by parts} with $\psi = e^{h\Delta} \phi_h$ cancels half of the term in $2 \nabla^2 U$ of \eqref{eq: sgld bias}. The remainder $h^{\frac{21}{11}}$ of \eqref{eq: sgld bias continuous} comes from moving the expectation of the gradient term from $\bar\pi_h$ to $\pi$: the vector $\nabla^2 U\, \nabla U - \nabla \Delta U$ grows linearly, and the moments of $\bar\pi_h$ are bounded up to the order $12$ of Assumption~\ref{ass: sg} only.

\subsection{Explicit coefficient in Wasserstein-1 error}
\label{subsec: w1}

The coefficients \eqref{eq: bias coefficient} and \eqref{eq: sgld coefficient} depend on the test function $f$ through $\nabla^2 \phi_0$. Since $\mathcal C$ and $\nabla^2 U$ are bounded and Lipschitz, \eqref{eq: representor poisson} writes the two coefficients through the Riesz representors of Section~\ref{subsec: riesz},
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)}{h} = \frac12\, \pi( f \psi_{\mathcal C} ) , \qquad
    \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\bar\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)}{h} = \frac12\, \pi\bigl( f\, ( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} ) \bigr) .
    \label{eq: coefficients riesz}
\end{equation}
The representor of the Hessian $\nabla^2 U$ is explicit. Since the vector $\nabla^2 U\, \nabla U - \nabla \Delta U$ is the gradient of $\frac12 |\nabla U|^2 - \Delta U$, whose mean under $\pi$ is $-\frac12 \pi(\Delta U)$, \eqref{eq: sgld integration by parts}, \eqref{eq: generator ibp} and the uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm: riesz} give
\begin{equation}
    \psi_{\nabla^2 U}(x) = \frac12\, |\nabla U(x)|^2 - \Delta U(x) + \frac12\, \pi(\Delta U) .
    \label{eq: psi hessian}
\end{equation}
With \eqref{eq: representor poisson}, the suprema of \eqref{eq: numerical bias} and \eqref{eq: sgld bias continuous} over $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty \Le 1$ give, for every $h \in (0,h_0]$,
\begin{equation}
    \Bigl\| \pi_h - \pi - \frac h2\, \psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi \Bigr\|_{\mathrm{KR}} \Le C h^2 \log\frac1h , \qquad
    \Bigl\| \bar\pi_h - \pi - \frac h2\, ( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} )\, \pi \Bigr\|_{\mathrm{KR}} \Le C h^{\frac{21}{11}} .
    \label{eq: kr expansion}
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi) = \norm{\pi_h - \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}}$ and $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi) = \norm{\bar\pi_h - \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}}$, \eqref{eq: kr expansion} gives
\begin{equation}
    \biggl| \mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi) - \frac h2\, \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} \biggr| \Le C h^2 \log\frac1h , \qquad
    \biggl| \mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi) - \frac h2\, \norm{( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} )\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} \biggr| \Le C h^{\frac{21}{11}} .
    \label{eq: w1 limits}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor: w1 limits}
    Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, the numerical invariant distribution $\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sglm} and the invariant distribution $\bar\pi_h$ of \eqref{eq: sgld} satisfy
    \begin{equation*}
        \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)}{h} = \frac12\, \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} , \qquad
        \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)}{h} = \frac12\, \norm{( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} )\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} .
    \end{equation*}
    Moreover, $\norm{\psi_{\nabla^2 U}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} > 0$, and $\norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} > 0$ if $\mathcal C(x)$ does not vanish identically. \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: w1 limits proof}.)}
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits} is nontrivial: it gives an accurate characterization of the coefficient of $h$ in the Wasserstein-1 error, as an equality. This makes it distinct from the mainstream coupling methods of numerical analysis, which bound the Wasserstein-1 error from above without identifying its coefficient.

Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits} also compares SGLM with SGLD through the representors: the limit of $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)/h$ is at most that of $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)/h$ exactly when
\begin{equation}
    \norm{( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} )\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} \Ge \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} .
    \label{eq: w1 comparison}
\end{equation}
This inequality fails in general: Lemma~\ref{lem: w1 counterexample} gives a gradient noise of large amplitude, supported away from the minimizer of $U$, that violates it.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem: w1 counterexample}
    Let $d = 1$, $U(x) = \frac12 x^2$ and $\hat g(x;\xi) = x + 10\, \xi\, a(x)$, where $\xi$ takes the values $1$ and $-1$ with probability $\frac12$ each and
    \begin{equation}
        a(x) := \exp\Bigl( 1 - \frac{1}{1 - 4 (|x| - 2)^2} \Bigr) \quad \text{if } \bigl| |x| - 2 \bigr| < \frac12 , \qquad a(x) := 0 \quad \text{otherwise} .
        \label{eq: shell noise}
    \end{equation}
    Then Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg} hold, and
    \begin{equation*}
        \frac12\, \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} \approx 1.230 > 1.115 \approx \frac12\, \norm{( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C} )\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} ,
    \end{equation*}
    so \eqref{eq: w1 comparison} fails. \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: w1 counterexample proof}.)}
\end{lemma}

With a batch of $P$ independent stochastic gradients, $\mathcal C(x)/P$ replaces the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$ of a single stochastic gradient. As $P$ grows, the limit of $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)/h$ tends to $0$, while that of $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)/h$ tends to $\frac12 \norm{\psi_{\nabla^2 U}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} > 0$; for a strongly convex potential, \eqref{eq: w1 comparison} holds once $P$ reaches an explicit threshold.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: batch threshold}
    Under Assumption~\ref{ass: U} with $R = 0$, that is, $\nabla^2 U(x) \succeq m I_d$ for every $x \in \mathbb R^d$, and Assumption~\ref{ass: sg}, let $\mathcal C(x)$ be the covariance of a single stochastic gradient. Then, for every batch size $P$ satisfying
    \begin{equation}
        P \Ge \frac{9\, \norm{\nabla^2 U}_\infty\, \norm{\mathcal C}_\infty}{m^2} = \frac{9}{m^2} \sup_{x\in\mathbb R^d} \norm{\nabla^2U(x)} \sup_{x\in\mathbb R^d} \norm{\mathcal C(x)},
        \label{eq: batch threshold}
    \end{equation}
    the limit of $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)/h$ for SGLM with a batch of size $P$ is at most that of $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)/h$ for SGLD with the same batch, that is, by Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits} with $\psi_{\mathcal C}/P$ in place of $\psi_{\mathcal C}$,
    \begin{equation}
        \Big\|\Big( \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \frac1P\psi_{\mathcal C} \Big) \pi\Big\|_{\mathrm{KR}} \Ge \frac1P\, \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} .
        \label{eq: w1 comparison batch}
    \end{equation}
    \emph{(Proof in Appendix~\ref{sec: batch threshold proof}.)}
\end{theorem}

The norm $\norm{\cdot}$ in \eqref{eq: batch threshold} is the operator norm of Section~\ref{subsec: notation}, so the lower bound of $P$ does not depend explicitly on the dimension $d$; it depends only on the eigenvalues of $\nabla^2 U(x)$ and $\mathcal C(x)$.

\section{Bias correction via the Riesz representors}
\label{sec: representors}

By \eqref{eq: coefficients riesz}, the numerical bias $\pi_h(f) - \pi(f)$ of SGLM equals $\frac h2\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ to first order in $h$, so it is removed once $\pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ is computed. Subtracting $\frac h2\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ from $\pi_h(f)$ requires the value of $\pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ for the given test function $f$, not the representor $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ itself. This section describes three approaches to bias correction. For the covariances of Section~\ref{subsec: explicit representors}, $\pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ is computed with the explicit representor $\psi_{\mathcal C}$. The Richardson--Romberg extrapolation of Section~\ref{subsec: extrapolation} requires only the time averages at the step sizes $h$ and $2h$. The shifting correction of Section~\ref{subsec: shifting} improves the samples themselves by solving the function $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$ from a variational form. Section~\ref{subsec: kr computation} describes the computation of the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norms that give the coefficients of the Wasserstein-1 errors. The three approaches also apply to SGLD, with $\bar\pi_h$ and $\psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C}$ in place of $\pi_h$ and $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ by \eqref{eq: coefficients riesz}, where $\psi_{\nabla^2 U}$ is explicit by \eqref{eq: psi hessian}; we focus on SGLM for simplicity.

\subsection{Covariances with an explicit representor}
\label{subsec: explicit representors}

Under Assumptions~\ref{ass: U} and \ref{ass: sg}, define the vector function $b_{\mathcal C}(x)$ by
\begin{equation}
    b_{\mathcal C}(x) := \mathcal C(x)\, \nabla U(x) - \operatorname{div} \mathcal C(x) ,
    \label{eq: representor field}
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{div}$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem: representor gradient}. An integration by parts against $\pi \propto e^{-U}$ gives, for $\omega$ as in \eqref{eq: riesz},
\begin{equation}
    \pi\bigl( \nabla^2 \omega : \mathcal C \bigr) = \pi\bigl( \nabla \omega \cdot b_{\mathcal C} \bigr) .
    \label{eq: representor ibp}
\end{equation}
When $b_{\mathcal C} = \nabla \psi$ for a function $\psi$ whose derivatives grow at most polynomially, \eqref{eq: generator ibp} and the uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm: riesz} give $\psi_{\mathcal C} = \psi - \pi(\psi)$. Table~\ref{tab: explicit representors} lists covariances and potentials for which $b_{\mathcal C}$ is a gradient.

With a constant $a = \sigma^2$, the row $a(U(x))\, I_d$ of Table~\ref{tab: explicit representors} gives $\psi_{\mathcal C} = \sigma^2 \bigl( U - \pi(U) \bigr)$. For a general covariance in dimension $d \Ge 2$, the field $b_{\mathcal C}$ is not a gradient and $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ has no closed form; the extrapolation of Section~\ref{subsec: extrapolation} and the energy \eqref{eq: extrapolated energy} of Section~\ref{subsec: shifting} do not require $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ directly.

\subsection{Richardson--Romberg extrapolation}
\label{subsec: extrapolation}

The term of order $h$ of the numerical bias of a given test function can also be removed by extrapolation in the step size, a method used for the invariant distribution of an ergodic diffusion \citep{lemaire2015invariant} and for SGLD \citep{durmus2016stochastic}. For $h \in (0, h_0/2]$ and a function $f$ with $\norm{\nabla f}_\infty < \infty$, \eqref{eq: numerical bias} with \eqref{eq: representor poisson} at the step sizes $h$ and $2h$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \biggl| \frac{\pi_{2h}(f) - \pi_h(f)}{h} - \frac12\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C}) \biggr| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty\, h \log\frac1h ,
    \label{eq: extrapolation sglm}
\end{equation}
so the coefficient $\frac12\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ is approximated by $(\pi_{2h}(f) - \pi_h(f))/h$, computed from the time averages of $f$ along runs of \eqref{eq: sglm} with the step sizes $h$ and $2h$. Subtracting $\pi_{2h}(f) - \pi_h(f)$ from $\pi_h(f)$ gives the approximation $2\pi_h(f) - \pi_{2h}(f)$ of $\pi(f)$, with
\begin{equation}
    \bigl| 2\pi_h(f) - \pi_{2h}(f) - \pi(f) \bigr| \Le C \norm{\nabla f}_\infty\, h^2 \log\frac1h .
    \label{eq: extrapolation estimate}
\end{equation}

Extrapolation corrects the expectations of given test functions but not the samples, whose laws remain $\pi_h$ and $\pi_{2h}$; it does not raise the order of the Wasserstein-1 error in \eqref{eq: w1 limits}. Moreover, \eqref{eq: extrapolation sglm} holds for each given test function and does not mean that the empirical samples of $\pi_h$ and $\pi_{2h}$ approximate $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ itself: for the gradient noise $\sigma \xi$ with $\xi \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$, $\psi_{\mathcal C} = \sigma^2 \bigl( U - \pi(U) \bigr)$ by Table~\ref{tab: explicit representors}, which is not read off the samples.

\subsection{Postprocessed shifting correction}
\label{subsec: shifting}

The extrapolation of Section~\ref{subsec: extrapolation} improves the expectations $\pi_h(f)$, but not the samples of SGLM themselves. We instead define a vector field that shifts the samples of SGLM to more accurate ones. The vector field is $h\, \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}$, with $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$ the solution of
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L \varphi_{\mathcal C} = \frac12\, \psi_{\mathcal C} , \qquad \pi(\varphi_{\mathcal C}) = 0 .
    \label{eq: shift potential}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq: generator ibp}, $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$ is the minimizer of the functional
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal E(\varphi) := \frac12\, \pi\bigl( |\nabla \varphi|^2 \bigr) + \frac12\, \pi\bigl( \psi_{\mathcal C}\, \varphi \bigr) , \qquad \pi(\varphi) = 0 ,
    \label{eq: shift energy}
\end{equation}
and since $\psi_{\mathcal C} \in L^2(\pi)$ and $\pi(\psi_{\mathcal C}) = 0$, the minimizer over the functions $\varphi$ with $\varphi, |\nabla \varphi| \in L^2(\pi)$ exists and is unique by the Poincar\'e inequality \eqref{eq: poincare} and the Riesz representation theorem. The shifting correction replaces a sample $Z \sim \pi_h$ of SGLM by the postprocessed sample
\begin{equation}
    Z + h\, \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}(Z) ,
    \label{eq: shifting correction}
\end{equation}
where the map $x \mapsto x + h\, \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}(x)$ is a postprocessor in the sense of \citep{vilmart2015postprocessed}, that is, a change of variables applied to the samples to raise the order for the invariant distribution. Formally, let $f$ be a smooth test function. A Taylor expansion of $f$ at $x$ and \eqref{eq: numerical bias} with \eqref{eq: representor poisson}, applied to $f$ and to $\nabla f \cdot \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}$, give
\begin{equation*}
    \pi_h\bigl( f(x + h \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}(x)) \bigr) = \pi(f) + \frac h2\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C}) + h\, \pi(\nabla f \cdot \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}) + \mathcal O\Bigl( h^2 \log\frac1h \Bigr) ,
\end{equation*}
where the computation is formal because $\nabla f \cdot \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}$ is not known to be Lipschitz.
By \eqref{eq: generator ibp} with $f_1 = f$ and $f_2 = \varphi_{\mathcal C}$, and by \eqref{eq: shift potential},
\begin{equation*}
    \pi(\nabla f \cdot \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}) = -\pi(f\, \mathcal L \varphi_{\mathcal C}) = -\frac12\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C}) ,
\end{equation*}
so the terms $\frac h2\, \pi(f \psi_{\mathcal C})$ and $h\, \pi(\nabla f \cdot \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C})$ cancel, and formally
\begin{equation*}
    \pi_h\bigl( f(x + h \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}(x)) \bigr) = \pi(f) + \mathcal O\Bigl( h^2 \log\frac1h \Bigr) .
\end{equation*}
The error of the postprocessed samples is thus formally of order $h^2 \log\frac1h$ for every test function at once, and the shifting correction formally raises the order of the Wasserstein-1 error in \eqref{eq: w1 limits} from $h$ to $h^2 \log\frac1h$. The rigorous study of the shifting correction is left for future work.

When $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ is explicit, as in Section~\ref{subsec: explicit representors}, $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$ is approximated by minimizing \eqref{eq: shift energy} over a class of functions, with $\pi$ replaced by the average over the empirical samples of $\pi_h$. In general, the energy \eqref{eq: shift energy} uses $\psi_{\mathcal C}$ only through $\frac12\, \pi(\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \varphi)$, the coefficient \eqref{eq: coefficients riesz} of the test function $\varphi$, which \eqref{eq: extrapolation sglm} approximates. Replacing $\frac12\, \pi(\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \varphi)$ by $(\pi_{2h}(\varphi) - \pi_h(\varphi))/h$, and $\pi$ by $\pi_h$ in $\frac12\, \pi(|\nabla \varphi|^2)$, gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal E_h(\varphi) := \frac12\, \pi_h\bigl( |\nabla \varphi|^2 \bigr) + \frac{\pi_{2h}(\varphi) - \pi_h(\varphi)}{h} .
    \label{eq: extrapolated energy}
\end{equation}
The energy $\mathcal E_h$ requires only the empirical samples of $\pi_h$ and $\pi_{2h}$, from runs of \eqref{eq: sglm} with the step sizes $h$ and $2h$, so it is completely data-driven: it involves neither $U$, $\nabla U$ nor $\mathcal C(x)$, and one minimization gives the shift. It does not change when a constant is added to $\varphi$, so its minimization needs no constraint on $\varphi$. A minimizer $\varphi$ of $\mathcal E_h$ satisfies $h\, \pi_h(\nabla f \cdot \nabla \varphi) = \pi_h(f) - \pi_{2h}(f)$ for every $f$, so the shift $x \mapsto x + h \nabla \varphi(x)$ formally carries $\pi_h(f)$ to the approximation $2\pi_h(f) - \pi_{2h}(f)$ of \eqref{eq: extrapolation estimate}, up to terms of order $h^2$, for every $f$ at once. The step size $h$ cannot be too small for the minimization to be stable: the fluctuation of the empirical averages that replace $\pi_{2h}(\varphi)$ and $\pi_h(\varphi)$ is divided by $h$ in \eqref{eq: extrapolated energy}, and the minimizer of $\mathcal E_h$ with these empirical averages fits this fluctuation.

\begin{remark}
The loss function $\mathcal E_h$ of \eqref{eq: extrapolated energy} is closely related to the loss in action matching \citep{neklyudov2023action}, which learns, from samples of a family of distributions $(q_t)_{t \in [0,1]}$, a vector field $\nabla s_t$ that transports them, by minimizing
\begin{equation*}
    q_0(s_0) - q_1(s_1) + \int_0^1 q_t\Bigl( \frac12\, |\nabla s_t|^2 + \partial_t s_t \Bigr)\, \D t
\end{equation*}
over functions $s_t(x)$. With $q_0 = \pi_{2h}$, $q_1 = \pi_h$, the function $s_t = h \varphi$ independent of $t$, and $q_t$ replaced by $\pi_h$ in the integral, this loss equals $h^2\, \mathcal E_h(\varphi)$. A loss of the same form, whose $2$ terms are expectations estimated by sampling, is also written in \citep{cao2022learning}. In these works, the vector field transports the samples between the given distributions, while the shifting correction applies it beyond them.

Since $\pi_{2h} - \pi_h$ is of order $h$, a minimizer $\varphi$ of \eqref{eq: extrapolated energy} formally gives $\pi_{2h}\bigl( f(x + h \nabla \varphi(x)) \bigr) = \pi_h(f) + \mathcal O(h^2)$, so the map $x \mapsto x + h \nabla \varphi(x)$ carries $\pi_{2h}$ to $\pi_h$, and the shifting correction applies the same map once more, from $\pi_h$ toward $\pi$. Formally, up to terms of order $h^2$, the shifting correction is thus the extrapolation at the time $2$ of the Wasserstein geodesic from $\pi_{2h}$ at the time $0$ to $\pi_h$ at the time $1$, the operation used in \citep{gallouet2024geodesic, gallouet2025metric} for time discretizations of Wasserstein gradient flows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Calculation of the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm}
\label{subsec: kr computation}

The coefficients of the Wasserstein-1 errors in Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits} are $\frac12 \norm{\psi\,\pi}_{\mathrm{KR}}$, with $\psi = \psi_{\mathcal C}$ for SGLM and $\psi = \psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C}$ for SGLD, so they require the norm itself and not only the representor. On a convex compact domain, with no flux through its boundary, the supremum that defines the norm in Section~\ref{subsec: notation} equals the least cost of a flux $F$ that carries the negative part of $\psi\pi$ onto its positive part \citep[(2)]{li2018parallel}:
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\psi\pi}_{\mathrm{KR}} = \min\Bigl\{ \int_{\mathbb R^d} |F(x)|\, \D x : \operatorname{div} F = -\psi\,\pi \Bigr\} .
    \label{eq: kr flux}
\end{equation}

For the computation, $\psi\,\pi$ is restricted to a box centered at the origin that carries all but a negligible part of $\pi$, with $n$ cells per side: the function $f$ of the supremum is placed at the centers of the cells, with forward differences of Euclidean norm at most $1$; the flux $F$ is placed on the faces; and $\psi\,\pi$ is replaced by its cell averages. The primal--dual iteration of \citet{li2018parallel} solves the two discrete problems together, and refining the grid approximates \eqref{eq: kr flux}.
The grid has $n^d$ cells, so the cost grows exponentially with the dimension: in $d=4$, $n = 100$ already gives $10^8$ cells. Computing Kantorovich--Rubinstein norms in high dimensions is intrinsically difficult, and it is possibly unnecessary in the current setting, because the Wasserstein-1 error is not a \emph{quantity of interest} in real computations: samples are used to estimate the expectations $\pi(f)$ of given test functions, and the bias of each of them is given to first order by the coefficient \eqref{eq: coefficients riesz}, which \eqref{eq: extrapolation sglm} approximates without the norm or the representor.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec: conclusion}

For a potential strongly convex outside a ball, this paper proves an MSE bound for SGLM, which contains that of its full gradient form LM, and gives the explicit coefficients of $h$ in the numerical bias and in the Wasserstein-1 error of SGLM and SGLD. The samples of \eqref{eq: sglm} do not form a Markov chain; the modified class of Section~\ref{sec: modified} moves the analysis to the Markovian form \eqref{eq: sglm markov}, and the discrete Poisson equation of \citep{lu2025mse, ye2026mse} converts its local weak error against the modified dynamics into the bound $\frac{1}{Nh} + \Sigma^2 h^2 + h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ of Theorem~\ref{thm: sglm mse}, with the bound $\frac{1}{Nh} + h^4 \log^2\frac1h$ for the full gradient. These bounds hold for Lipschitz test functions, since the mean of the remainder of the local weak error is half an order smaller than its size. The stochastic gradient error and the discretization error thus separate for an integrator of the overdamped dynamics, which is comparable with SG-UBU \citep{lu2025mse} and does not hold for \eqref{eq: sgld}.

The numerical bias of SGLM is of first order in $h$, with the coefficient \eqref{eq: bias coefficient} given by the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$ of the gradient noise. For \eqref{eq: sgld}, the same discrete Poisson equation gives the coefficient \eqref{eq: sgld coefficient}, which carries the Hessian $\nabla^2 U(x)$ besides the covariance $\mathcal C(x)$. This contribution of the discretization error of EM remains at every batch size, while the coefficient of SGLM is divided by the batch size. In both coefficients, the gradient noise and the discretization error enter linearly through $\nabla^2 \phi_0$: the Poisson equation describes the structure of the numerical bias of both stochastic gradient integrators, beyond its role in the upper bounds on the MSE. Both coefficients come with remainders, of order $h^2 \log\frac1h$ for SGLM (Theorem~\ref{thm: numerical bias}) and $h^{\frac{21}{11}}$ for SGLD (Theorem~\ref{thm: sgld bias}), for every Lipschitz test function.

Through the Riesz representors of $\mathcal C(x)$ and $\nabla^2 U(x)$, the coefficients of $h$ in the Wasserstein-1 errors are explicit: $\mathcal W_1(\pi_h, \pi)/h$ and $\mathcal W_1(\bar\pi_h, \pi)/h$ converge to $\frac12 \norm{\psi_{\mathcal C}\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}}$ and $\frac12 \norm{(\psi_{\nabla^2 U} + \psi_{\mathcal C})\, \pi}_{\mathrm{KR}}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor: w1 limits}), as a consequence of the convergence \eqref{eq: kr expansion} of the normalized biases $(\pi_h - \pi)/h$ and $(\bar\pi_h - \pi)/h$ in the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm. For a strongly convex potential, the coefficient of SGLM is at most that of SGLD once the batch size reaches a threshold linear in $\sup_x \norm{\mathcal C(x)}$, with no explicit dependence on the dimension (Theorem~\ref{thm: batch threshold}), and Lemma~\ref{lem: w1 counterexample} shows that the order can be reversed at small batch sizes.

The coefficients also lead to approaches to bias correction in Section~\ref{sec: representors}: the explicit coefficient, Richardson--Romberg extrapolation and the shifting correction. The shifting correction replaces a sample $Z \sim \pi_h$ of SGLM by $Z + h \nabla \varphi_{\mathcal C}(Z)$ and formally removes the first order bias for all test functions at once. It is completely data-driven, since $\varphi_{\mathcal C}$ is approximated by minimizing the energy \eqref{eq: extrapolated energy} with the samples of SGLM at the step sizes $h$ and $2h$.

The numerical experiments of Section~\ref{sec: numerics} illustrate the coefficients, the bias correction and the comparison with SGLD. The evaluated coefficients of the numerical bias and of the Wasserstein-1 errors approach the exact ones as $h$ decreases, and the bias corrections reduce the numerical bias of SGLM. In Bayesian logistic regression on MNIST, SGLM has a smaller estimated MSE than SGLD for all test functions and batch sizes, and the reduction grows with the batch size, mainly through a smaller bias, in line with the coefficient of SGLM being divided by the batch size.

Four extensions are possible. The first is variance reduction: with variance-reduced gradients, the first order term of the bias of SG-UBU becomes of second order once $h$ is below a threshold \citep{lu2025mse}, and the same construction applied to \eqref{eq: sglm} would give an integrator of the overdamped dynamics with a bias of second order. The second is the remainders of \eqref{eq: w1 limits}: the factor $\log\frac1h$ for SGLM, which comes from the derivative of order $3$ of the discrete Poisson solution of a Lipschitz test function, and the order $h^{\frac{21}{11}}$ for SGLD, which comes from the moments of its invariant distribution and could be enhanced to $h^2 \log\frac1h$. The third is the underdamped Langevin dynamics, where a modified class for its stochastic gradient integrators would extend the analysis to that setting. The fourth is a rigorous study of the shifting correction, whose order $h^2 \log\frac1h$ is formal, together with the stability of the minimization of \eqref{eq: extrapolated energy}, in which the fluctuation of the empirical averages is divided by $h$.
